# Malaspina — transport and overseas districts

Survey XI · 970 settlements · 2,182 transport segments: 782 railway, 1,293 road, 106 sea and one proposed alignment.

## Travel and interchange

Shipping and railway services. Lines show working shipping corridors and named calls, not every departure or a compulsory stop for every ship. Ocean routes, coastal services and island supply passages form one connected port network. Fuel and water depots import or collect supplies; they do not imply local petroleum fields.

Otranto and Morholt have connected commercial corridors with separate national operators and border interchanges. Vesalius retains western, northern, southern and Eastern Marches systems. Overland roads and coastal shipping link those systems; no continuous transcontinental Vesalian railway or operating direct Karsenne–Calvernis line is charted. The Lelene–Bellisio industrial branch in southern Calvernis remains separate, with road transfer to the broader system.

Overseas districts. Coastal dependencies and ten outer-island polities and colonial administrations are charted. Seven outer societies are self-governing; Talasca, Ordelune and Edrask are overseas dependencies of Rovessara, Ostrevain and Rovengard. Unshaded isolated and polar islands have no settled sovereignty recorded; this does not establish that every landing is unused. Astrellac’s Cortessia, Vasselac and Rovellisse harbours provide the eastern Otrantan staging chain; imported bunker fuel, water stores and repairs support onward journeys to Vesalius and Morholt.

Polar administration. Boreal and Austral Land have no recorded permanent population or generally recognised sovereign partition. Seasonal exploration, whaling and refuge claims may exist without settled administration or dependable passenger services.

Connected railway corridors do not mean a single operator, uniform gauge or through passenger ticket. Border inspections, wagon handling, local timetables and damage can require changes. No gauge dimensions or new express journey times are assigned. The original Eastern Marches measured routes retain their distances and times.

The 72 charted harbours form a connected sea network. Smaller uncharted fishing landings also exist. A drawn sea line is a navigable service corridor, not a guarantee of safety or a claim that every passing ship must stop. Northern sailings can close with seasonal ice.

## Island administrations

### Bellacosta Cantons

Separate canton harbour dependencies; local fishing rights and harbour dues remain with the charter communities.

Charted harbours: Vessantine

### Rovessara

Republican overseas districts administered through elected harbour councils and Rovessaran customs officers.

Charted harbours: Marcavisse

### Cavressa Principalities

Dependencies of individual coastal principalities, linked by a limited pilotage compact rather than a new island kingdom.

Charted harbours: Monteliva

### Veylac

Veylac customs and lighthouse districts protecting its southern approaches.

Charted harbours: Cavresset

### Ossavren successor territories

A dependency of Neravisse’s municipal charter, not territory governed by a restored Ossavren crown.

Charted harbours: Cavralto

### Seravelle Littoral

Astrellac’s chartered island dependencies within the Seravelle return. Resident councils administer land and fisheries; Astrellac supplies customs officers, escorts and bonded fuel depots. The other Seravelle courts remain independent.

Charted harbours: Cortessia, Vasselac, Rovellisse

### Ostrevain

Royal coastal dependency with a governor, local fishing communities and an agricultural resupply station.

Charted harbours: Villessia

### Vaulcerre Basin Leagues

Offshore charter communities of the basin leagues, sharing pilots and navigation dues without a unified sovereign.

Charted harbours: Cernavie

### Veldrassen

A provincial coastal dependency with fishing villages and navigation stations supplied from Valdorelle.

Charted harbours: Small landing places and seasonal stations; no principal town added to the chart.

### Rovengard

Royal island districts with resident councils, coastal patrols and Halsavik supply contracts.

Charted harbours: Veltrund

### Halskert

Republican grain-shipping dependencies with elected port boards and permanent fishing settlements.

Charted harbours: Seldren

### Varneselle Estates

Varkessant’s port-charter dependencies; the mainland estate courts retain their separate jurisdictions.

Charted harbours: Cersund

### Galdresk

Wardenship navigation and shelter claims. Seasonal landings and small service crews do not imply a dense iceward population.

Charted harbours: Small landing places and seasonal stations; no principal town added to the chart.

### Dreissen Wardholds

Claims of adjacent wardholds, maintained by fishing visits and seasonal convoy shelters rather than continuous occupation.

Charted harbours: Small landing places and seasonal stations; no principal town added to the chart.

### Haldrevik Concessions

Island shore communities under Haldrevik charter protection. Concession leases cover named working sites, not ownership of all inhabitants.

Charted harbours: Rovensac

### Gavrel

Dependencies of individual march houses under a common coastal supply compact; no unified Gavrel navy or crown is implied.

Charted harbours: Loravise

### Varnelle

Delta-authority island districts with customs houses, repair yards and provisioning farms.

Charted harbours: Cervallune

### Rivessac Coast

A dependency of the coastal port commune, governed by its harbour charter and resident island councillors.

Charted harbours: Vallarive

### Kelbrun

Council-administered island dependency with plantation suppliers, fisheries and bonded stores.

Charted harbours: Marcellune

### Vardol

Northern crown claims maintained by lighthouse crews and seasonal naval stores. Remote interiors are not continuously occupied.

Charted harbours: Small landing places and seasonal stations; no principal town added to the chart.

### Averholt

Claims administered by the northern coastal province; fishing landings and seasonal shelters receive supplies from Bravessac.

Charted harbours: Small landing places and seasonal stations; no principal town added to the chart.

### Tervayne

Overseas supply and navigation districts maintained by Tervayne’s maritime administration and resident port councils.

Charted harbours: Ostrelac

### Bressavelle Marches

Chartered island lordships tied to the western marches by supply contracts; Tervayne has commercial privileges, not sovereignty.

Charted harbours: Lorvesset

### Vallessia Cantons

Dependencies of individual southern cantons, governed through resident councils and grain-shipping charters.

Charted harbours: Cervelune

## Outer ocean chains

See [ISLAND-CHAINS.md](ISLAND-CHAINS.md) for the ten chain administrations, existing island societies and separate census coverage. Two charted harbours per chain support feeder boats to smaller landings. One local road links each principal harbour to its island market. No oceanic railway is implied. Northern and austral sailings are weather- and season-dependent; refuge harbours do not all carry full bunker stocks.

## Census and chronology

The revision52 atlas contains 970 settlements and 2,182 transport segments, including 106 sea passages joining 72 harbours. Ten outer-island regions cover 63 additional islands: seven self-governing polities and three colonial administrations. Talasca belongs to Rovessara, Ordelune to Ostrevain and Edrask to Rovengard, with separate geographic returns. The corrected census is 1,223,820,000 across 43 disjoint groups; the added 14,620,000 outer-island residents were omitted from earlier coverage. Existing homeland populations, capacities, coastlines, mainland borders and all earlier routes remain unchanged. This is existing-world clarification, not new growth, migration, colonisation, construction or elapsed story time. Seven railway systems remain; no transcontinental Vesalian railway or direct Karsenne–Calvernis line is opened. Read ISLAND-CHAINS.md and TRANSPORT-REFERENCE.md. Earlier survey counts and blank outer-island assumptions are superseded.
