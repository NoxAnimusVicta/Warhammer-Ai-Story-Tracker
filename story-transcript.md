## 003 — Assistant · Opening scene

At first, there is a heartbeat.

A slow, powerful rhythm, muffled by warm liquid. Beneath it runs another sound: a measured mechanical pulse, interrupted every seventh beat by a faint metallic click.

Then the world tilts.

Something immense strikes the outside of the chamber. The liquid shudders. Beyond a curved surface clouded with condensation, white light becomes orange, then black. A succession of smaller impacts travels through the enclosing metal.

Silence follows.

The mechanical pulse continues.

One by one, narrow green lights illuminate the chamber. Their glow catches strands of white hair floating in the milky fluid. Small hands. Bare limbs. Dark tubes recessed into the walls.

A valve opens beneath your feet.

Warmth drains away.

Air enters with the smell of hot metal, wet earth and crushed vegetation. The supports beneath your body remain warm as the fluid falls around them. Somewhere outside, glass breaks, each fragment striking the ground with a separate, remarkably distinct note.

Three heavy catches withdraw.

The lid rises.

Beyond it stands the skeleton of a vast glasshouse.

Iron ribs arch overhead, some intact, others folded beneath the weight of climbing vegetation. The pod has torn through their canopy and come to rest at an angle in a shattered planting bed. Broken panes hang among leaves broad enough to cover your body. Through the opening above, evening light stains a low ceiling of ash the colour of old copper.

A severed vine drips thick yellow sap onto the pod’s scorched flank.

Nothing hurts.

The chamber’s machinery still turns beneath you. Its vibration passes through the supporting cradle: a steady rotation, a smaller oscillation, the recurring click. On the raised lid, tightly inscribed grooves hold a dim violet sheen. That light survives even where the outer metal has blackened.

Outside, pale roots have pushed through a tiled floor. They spread around a dry fountain whose central figure has lost its head and both hands. Little bronze plaques stand crookedly in the planting beds, covered in marks for which you have no meaning.

Farther away, the glasshouse opens onto a broken terrace.

Beyond that lies an enormous green descent.

Jungle fills the valley, its upper branches moving in a wind that has not yet reached the ruins. A narrow railway cuts across the slope below. In the distance, buildings crowd a strip of dark water beneath smoking chimneys. Points of amber light emerge between their roofs.

A deep note carries from that direction.

Another answers it, lower and farther away.

Near the foot of the pod, something rustles.

Three pale creatures emerge from beneath a broken tile. Each is no longer than a finger, with a translucent shell and many delicate legs. Their bodies darken where the draining nutrient fluid touches them. They cluster at its edge, thin mouthparts working against the stone.

More movement stirs beneath the tile.

Then a different sound rises from the valley.

Metal wheels, turning over joints in a track.

A small vehicle travels along the railway below, trailing a ragged thread of exhaust. Three upright figures ride upon it. A hooded lamp sways beside the foremost one; long tools rattle against its side. Another figure holds a narrow object of wood and dark metal across its knees.

Their voices reach the glasshouse as unfamiliar sounds.

One arm lifts toward the broken roof.

The vehicle begins to slow.

Inside the pod, the last of the warm fluid slips beneath the cradle. The chamber stands open to the cooling air. Nothing fastens your limbs.

At its base, the pale creatures continue to gather.

## 006 — Assistant · Outside the pod

Your fingers close around the pod’s rim. The metal is warm and slick, but your grip holds. A pull brings your shoulders above the opening; another shifts your weight over its side.

The ground arrives unevenly.

One foot meets a thick root. The other slips against the curved edge of a tile, sending you sideways. Your hand catches the planting bed before you fall. Mortar crumbles beneath your fingers.

For a few moments, standing is an arrangement that keeps changing: the weight of your head, the unfamiliar distance between your feet, the slight pull of the sloping floor. Each correction becomes smaller. Soon you can remain upright without holding anything.

The pale creatures scatter at your footfalls. They disappear beneath the broken tile, leaving trembling rings in the spilled fluid.

From outside, the pod is larger than the chamber made it seem. Blackened casing surrounds a pale inner shell. One side lies buried in soil; the other has crushed the planting bed’s low wall. Heat rises from its underside. The open chamber remains within reach, softly illuminated.

Everything beyond it demands attention at once.

Sap falls through leaves. Small bodies move inside hollow roots. The distant wheels strike their track with a rhythm quite unlike the machinery behind you. Beneath the sharp smells of scorched metal and torn vegetation lies something sweet and decaying.

The details are distinct. Their significance is not.

A clicking sound comes from a dark cluster of leaves. It stops, begins again, then resolves into a thin scrap of metal swinging against an iron rib. Elsewhere, something rustles without becoming visible. There is too much unfamiliar movement to separate harmless activity from danger with any confidence.

Near your feet, broad roots provide patches of firm footing between the glass. Farther into the building, vegetation thickens around the dry fountain. Its stone basin offers a low obstruction behind which much of your body would disappear.

The terrace is more exposed. Through its broken frontage, you can see several steps descending before the plants conceal the rest. Whether they still reach the railway is impossible to tell from here.

Below, the wheel noise ends with a squeal.

The small vehicle has stopped.

One of its three occupants stands, bracing a hand against the seated figure beside it. The hooded lamp turns uphill. Its light passes across a retaining wall, catches the underside of an enormous leaf, and climbs toward the wrecked glasshouse.

A voice calls out.

The sounds carry a questioning rise, but no meaning you can recognise.

Behind you, the pod continues its steady mechanical pulse. Ahead, the lamp pauses upon the torn roof.

## 009 — Assistant · First contact

In the green light spilling from the pod, your skin gleams with drying fluid. Fingers, wrists, elbows. Below, the figures have the same arrangement of limbs, though theirs disappear beneath layers of dark material.

You cross the terrace toward them.

The first steps hold. Farther down, roots have lifted the stone into uneven ridges. One tread breaks beneath your weight, its outer edge tumbling away into the plants. Your hand closes around the surviving railing. Rust flakes between your fingers as the falling stone strikes the slope below.

The lamp snaps toward the sound.

Three faces turn upward.

You continue down the remaining steps, emerging from the vegetation near the railway. Here the air smells of oil, hot machinery and old smoke. The vehicle’s engine beats unevenly behind its metal housing.

You make the sound the voice made.

Its rising shape comes back readily. The smaller movements do not. Breath catches between unfamiliar syllables; the call emerges stretched and rough, its ending abruptly swallowed.

The standing figure answers immediately.

A short, sharp sound.

One hand rises, palm facing you.

Behind him, another occupant lifts the long wooden object to a shoulder. A narrow metal opening turns toward your approach. The third remains beside the engine controls, looking from you to the broken roof above.

There are perhaps twelve paces between you and the vehicle.

Up close, the standing figure has deep creases beside his mouth and short grey bristles along his jaw. His coat is patched at both elbows. A strip of stained cloth binds two fingers on the hand holding the lamp. Small pieces of glass glitter in the seams of his heavy boots.

The light moves over your bare limbs, then settles on your face.

He becomes very still.

His gaze lifts briefly toward the glasshouse. He says something over his shoulder. The reply comes quickly from the figure holding the wooden object, but neither voice offers words you understand.

The man with the lamp steps forward twice.

His raised palm remains between you.

This time, when he speaks, the sound is slower and quieter. He waits a moment, watching your face, then lowers his free hand to touch his own chest.

“Veskan.”

He repeats the gesture.

“Veskan.”

Behind him, the metal opening remains pointed your way. The engine rattles, and a moth circles through the lamplight between you.

## 012 — Assistant · A repeated name

Your first step carries you forward and slightly across the track. The second brings the man’s patched coat into line with the narrow metal opening.

Behind him, the weapon shifts—and stops. Its bearer lowers the end a little rather than keep it directed through his companion.

The man with the lamp notices. His eyes move from your feet to your raised hand, then briefly toward the person behind him.

Your palm remains open.

“Veskan.”

This time, the sound comes out clearly.

The separate movements fit together: breath, tongue, the brief closing of your lips. When you repeat it, the resemblance to his pronunciation is striking.

His brows draw together.

You touch your chest again.

For a moment, he watches without speaking. Then his raised hand presses gently forward through the air, still several paces away: a firm repetition of the stopping gesture.

He speaks quietly to the others. The weapon stays lowered just enough to clear his body. Its bearer’s hands remain tight around it.

The engine cuts out.

In the sudden quiet, leaves scrape against the ruined glasshouse above. Somewhere beneath the railway, water trickles through a culvert.

The man turns his full attention back to you.

He touches his own chest.

“Veskan.”

His fingertips tap the same place twice. Then he extends that hand toward you, palm upward, and makes a different sound—a short word with a rising end.

He waits.

The lamp illuminates the space between you: rails polished by passing wheels, crushed black stone, your bare feet. Beyond his shoulder, the third occupant leans against the stopped vehicle to get a better view of you.

Veskan repeats his name once more, touching himself as he says it. His open hand returns toward you.

He seems to be trying to distinguish the two of you.

## 014 — Assistant · An offer of cloth

“Veskan.”

You point at him.

His expression changes slightly. The tight crease between his brows eases, and he touches his chest once in acknowledgement.

Then your finger turns inward.

He watches you begin to speak, hesitate, and fall silent. Your small nod leaves him looking at you for another moment.

He does not supply a name.

Instead, he turns his head and speaks to the person beside the vehicle’s controls. A short exchange follows. The weapon-bearer interrupts, glancing toward the ruined glasshouse. Veskan answers more sharply, then holds out his free hand without looking away from you.

The third occupant reaches beneath the bench.

What emerges is a heavy, folded length of grey cloth, darkened along one edge. They pass it down to him. He shifts the lamp onto a projecting bracket beside the track, leaving both hands free.

Slowly, he unfolds the cloth.

He drapes it around his own shoulders for a moment, drawing the edges together over his chest. Then he removes it and holds it open toward you.

A demonstration.

The fabric smells of oil, old smoke and human bodies. Its lower edge hangs almost to the stones.

Veskan says a short word in a gentler voice. He remains where he is, holding the cloth within view rather than coming closer.

Behind him, the other man keeps his weapon low. His attention moves repeatedly between you and the dark vegetation above the steps.
