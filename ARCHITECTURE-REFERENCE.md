# Galahad’s monumental architecture

Price review: **11/10/0068 AC43**. Year 67 AC43 reference bands remain the baseline; no blanket new-year inflation is enacted. Apply recorded regional conditions and actual invoices, not automatic repricing.

Current design clarification · Constant Year 67 prices, reviewed at return

Galahad’s unbuilt Veyrasse designs aim at nationally recognisable architectural wonders: immense war memorials incorporating walk-through mausoleums, names of the dead cut into stone, sculptural halls, towers and elevated galleries. At least one integrates a working aqueduct, reservoirs and separate distribution to nearby crops, town supply and fountains. The intended scale is comparable to the great works of human civil engineering, including an Empire State Building-sized or larger breadth or mass. No commission or construction has occurred.

Existing structural calculations passed academic examination. The earlier record did not establish a complete site, measured water source or final bill of quantities. The player’s clarification establishes ambition and function; illustrative dimensions and prices remain planning assumptions.

Current-price planning range: **30–80 million lorrats**, with **50 million** as an illustrative central case. A version combining enormous breadth with skyscraper-like height could reach **100–200 million or more**, requiring its own design and site assessment. A 300–500 m by 100–180 m complex with principal structures 100–180 m high was an example, not a fixed drawing specification. The aqueduct route, geology, water yield and head, land rights, structural materials and level of ornament materially change the cost.

Assumes accessible materials, suitable foundations and a manageable water route. Borrowing costs and a separate Hoover-scale dam are excluded. Waterworks can open before all decorative work is complete. A 10–20-year programme was suggested, not contracted. Water supply, irrigation and ornamental branches must be hydraulically feasible and kept separate from burial spaces; waste disposal and maintenance need their own provision.
