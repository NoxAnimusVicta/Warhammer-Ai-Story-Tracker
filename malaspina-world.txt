Malaspina is an isolated human death world of ancient industrial cities, fortified aristocratic estates, and immense wildernesses. Its inhabitants live beneath the recurring threat of technologically superior xenos who use the planet as a hunting preserve. Human civilisation endures through heavy industry, disciplined military service, locally maintained technology, and jealously guarded traditions of psychic practice.

Founded by human settlers during the Dark Age of Technology, Malaspina lost interstellar contact during the Age of Strife. It was never brought into Imperial Compliance and has no surviving Imperial administration, Mechanicus establishment, Ecclesiarchy or recognisable Imperial creed. Its founding civilisation collapsed through isolation and repeated local catastrophes; present societies rebuilt unevenly from the remains. This history is campaign background, not knowledge already possessed by the subject.

Three principal continents divide its inhabited surface: Otranto, Morholt, and Vesalius. Each supports distinct human societies shaped by its terrain, resources, and exposure to the hunters. No government commands the entire planet. Authority belongs to competing city-states, hereditary houses, military strongholds, and industrial oligarchies whose territories overlap through treaties, marriages, debts, and longstanding claims.

Otranto holds the greatest concentration of population and industry. Jagged mountain chains enclose broad agricultural basins, while rivers connect densely settled valleys. Its cities climb fortified terraces: ivory palaces and copper domes overlook manufactoria, workers’ districts, rail terminals, and smoking foundries. Monumental aqueducts cross crowded avenues. Armoured trains pass through mountain tunnels and over immense viaducts, carrying grain, ore, troops, and machinery between defended settlements.

Otranto’s cultivated districts depend upon extensive drainage works, pumping stations, reservoirs, and maintained perimeter defences. Beyond these enclosures, forests advance rapidly across cleared ground. Agricultural wealth confers considerable political power, and disputes over water, transport routes, and food exports are frequent causes of conflict.

Morholt is colder, more heavily forested, and more sparsely settled. Towering conifers cover deep valleys beneath glacial mountains. Snowbound fortress cities surround coal-fired generating stations, district-heating plants, mineral workings, and sheltered industrial sites. Heated glasshouses provide food within their walls. Tracked vehicles and armoured supply trains connect the larger settlements, while distant outposts depend on seasonal expeditions.

The wilderness of Morholt contains extensive remnants of earlier human occupation. Marble colonnades stand among enormous roots; collapsed transit lines disappear beneath ferns and luminous fungi. Some ruins contain corroded boilers, seized pumps and collapsed industrial workings from earlier local settlements. Any surviving fragments of the original interstellar colony are exceptionally rare, broken and effectively unrecognisable; no functioning advanced installation is established. Others have become dens for native predators or sites frequented by the alien hunters. Local troops specialise in reconnaissance, tracking, survival, and combat beneath the forest canopy.

Vesalius is a continent of volcanic uplands, dense jungles, and violent coastal weather. Steaming rivers descend through black basalt terraces. Offshore stone pillars and rugged cliffs shelter deep natural harbours. Human settlement concentrates along the coasts, where fortified ports support shipyards, fisheries, mineral exports, and maritime trade.

Vesalian cities rise in crowded terraces above their waterfronts. Copper roofs, stone mansions, furnace chimneys, and bronze navigation beacons overlook drydocks and armoured cargo vessels. Inland, flowering vegetation covers abandoned airfields, overgrown terraces and the weathered foundations of forgotten settlements. Expeditions seek mineral deposits and recoverable conventional machinery, supported by small settlements whose survival depends on regular coastal supply.

Malaspina’s ecology makes permanent habitation an undertaking requiring constant labour. Large predators stalk livestock, patrols, and industrial clearings. Some possess heavy natural plating; others hunt through heightened sensitivity to vibration, heat, or scent. Seasonal fungal blooms threaten stored food and exposed lungs. Aggressive vegetation invades roads, drainage channels, and foundations. Waterborne parasites require reliable treatment and filtration.

These hazards sustain entire professions. Perimeter crews clear vegetation, military engineers maintain transport corridors, and specialist workers inspect filters, reservoirs, and food stores. A settlement’s security depends as much on functioning machinery and dependable supplies as on its weapons.

Human culture combines elaborate courtly traditions with industrial militarism. Noble households maintain formal uniforms, heraldic devices, genealogical records, and private military forces. Officers wear high collars, long coats, polished armour, and carefully displayed service distinctions. Ivory, deep green, bronze, and gold feature prominently among Otranto’s wealthy households, while regional forces adopt clothing suited to their environments.

Prestige also belongs to skilled artificers, engineers, physicians, and scholars. Patronage of a foundry, academy, hospital, or public conservatory demonstrates wealth and authority. Art, music, ornamental craftsmanship, and monumental architecture flourish wherever resources permit. Beneath that splendour, much of the population works in demanding conditions, bound to employers and ruling families through inherited obligations, debt, or dependence upon protected accommodation.

Human technology is unevenly distributed, with its most capable industries and militaries broadly equivalent to the First and Second World Wars of ancient Terra. Factories produce steel, machine tools, electrical equipment, industrial chemicals, combustion engines and conventional armoured vehicles. Coal and oil power thermal stations, while suitable regions exploit hydroelectricity. Wired telephones, telegraph networks and radio support communications. These comparisons are player-facing references, not local terminology. Native industry cannot manufacture nuclear reactors, atomic weapons, energy weapons, autonomous war machines, powered armour, advanced force fields or functioning STC systems. Rare functional hunter captures are separate from this manufacturing ceiling.

A wealthy household can equip its retainers with well-made rifles, submachine guns, machine guns, mortars and artillery, supported by tanks, armoured cars and military trucks. Poorer provincial levies rely on older firearms and limited ammunition. Steel helmets and occasional conventional protective plates do not confer Imperial carapace protection. Propeller-driven aircraft operate from defended airfields, and steel steamships and diesel vessels serve the coasts. Access to fuel, replacement components, trained personnel and transport determines military strength. Native humanity has no working spacecraft or orbital infrastructure. No usable lasguns, bolters, plasma weapons or other Imperial military relics are established. Exceptionally rare ancient human offworld debris is broken and effectively unrecognisable; it does not imply an intact Imperial arsenal or accessible technical archive.

Rare captured hunter weapons and devices circulate as guarded trophies, military assets and objects of study. Earlier generations of human soldiers and scholars have already learned to operate some examples and partially understand a few systems. Limited repairs, adaptations and cannibalisation are possible where knowledge and parts survive; reliable manufacture of their advanced cores, power sources and specialised components is beyond the current industrial base. These are genuine functional exceptions to the native technology ceiling, not Imperial relics. No particular device is assigned to the player.

Psychic abilities and Warp phenomena are recognised features of life. Their interpretation varies between societies. Some communities place psykers under religious supervision; others employ them through scholarly institutions, hereditary service, or secretive circles of practitioners. Divination, warding, telekinesis, and ritual binding are studied with differing degrees of understanding and success.

Experiments combining machinery and psychic effects exist, usually within heavily guarded workshops or secluded sanctuaries. Such devices remain rare, temperamental, and dependent on particular practitioners. Their mechanical components remain within the local industrial ceiling; their anomalous effects come from psychic practice, not concealed advanced engineering. No reliable industrial substitute for advanced weapons or power systems is established. Records are fragmented, discoveries are closely guarded, and rival traditions frequently disagree over the causes of their successes and failures. Psychic practice offers real capabilities while carrying the dangers inherent in contact with the Warp.

The hunters are an original regional spacefaring xenos species, inspired by the Yautja as a player-facing reference, not a canonical 40K faction or a crossover identification. Their reach encompasses Malaspina and nearby systems: small beside the Imperium, overwhelmingly vast to local human societies. Their name, detailed biology and exact weapon specifications remain unrevealed.

The alien hunters operate across all three continents. They arrive from offworld in advanced craft and deploy sophisticated armour, concealment systems, sensors, and weapons. Their expeditions target dangerous wildlife as well as humans. Decorated soldiers, experienced wilderness fighters, and powerful psykers can attract particular attention.

The humans experience their presence as a continuing war punctuated by raids, disappearances, ambushes, and destructive interventions. The hunters treat human resistance as part of the preserve’s value. Their activity varies between expeditions: some pursue individual prey, others attack military formations or interfere with particular installations.

They maintain sites in remote regions and respond aggressively to developments that threaten their access to the planet. Attempts to follow their craft with optical observation or experimental radio detection, approach their remote sites, or organise sustained attacks against their facilities have drawn retaliation. They would resist a future challenge to their offworld access; native humans have no established orbital programme. Their presence is intermittent and their surveillance imperfect, allowing human societies considerable freedom within a wider condition of confinement.

Malaspina is held in enforced technological stagnation. Human ingenuity produces advances, but recurrent hunter attacks destroy workshops, infrastructure, records and skilled communities, while recovery operations reclaim or destroy captured equipment. Concentrated alien arsenals, conspicuous use and threatening research attract particular attention. Losses repeatedly offset gains; permanent war preparations and death-world maintenance consume the resources needed for sustained development. Even without the hunters, survival would demand substantial labour. Raids are intermittent locally, but their cumulative pressure is planetary and persistent. Imperfect surveillance permits concealed caches and scattered expertise to survive. This is an established historical condition, not a guarantee that every future advance must be erased regardless of player action.

The shared threat has produced alliances, mutual defence arrangements, and exchanges of military knowledge. It has also encouraged secrecy, appeasement, and accusations of collaboration. Rival states continue to compete for resources and territory, each convinced that control of its own institutions is essential to survival.

Malaspina remains a divided, inhabited world with substantial industrial resources, established cultures, decayed ruins, and vast territories beyond reliable human control. Its cities preserve wealth and learning behind formidable defences, while the wilderness and the skies remain contested. The technology ceiling describes native human civilisation at the opening of the campaign, not the hunters or Bile. Captured hunter equipment exists in local hands but remains external to reproducible native industry and is not pre-awarded player salvage. Future discoveries and inventions require events, resources and learning in play.
