Malaspina is an isolated, Earth-like human world of ancient industrial cities, fortified aristocratic estates and immense wildernesses, appropriated as an alien hunting preserve. Its system lies in Imperium Nihilus, beyond the Great Rift. Its temperate seas, fertile soils and varied climates once offered favourable conditions for settlement. Engineered wildlife and recurring xenos interventions now make life outside defended districts dangerous. Human civilisation endures through industry, collective defence and guarded traditions of the older arts.

Founded during humanity’s Golden Age of Technology, Malaspina suffered an early local collapse of its interstellar infrastructure late in that age. Its inhabitants had regressed to conventional industrial technology before the hunters appropriated the system. Wider isolation during the Age of Strife completed the loss of contact. It was never brought into Imperial Compliance and has no surviving Imperial administration, Mechanicus establishment, Ecclesiarchy or recognisable Imperial creed.

Three principal continents divide its inhabited surface: Otranto, Morholt, and Vesalius. Each supports distinct human societies shaped by its terrain, resources, and exposure to the hunters. No government commands the entire planet or any of its three principal continents. Authority belongs to competing city-states, hereditary houses, military strongholds, and industrial oligarchies whose territories overlap through treaties, marriages, debts, and longstanding claims.

Otranto holds the greatest concentration of population and industry. Jagged mountain chains enclose broad agricultural basins, while rivers connect densely settled valleys. Its cities climb fortified terraces: ivory palaces and copper domes overlook manufactoria, workers’ districts, rail terminals, and smoking foundries. Monumental aqueducts cross crowded avenues. Armoured trains pass through mountain tunnels and over immense viaducts, carrying grain, ore, troops, and machinery between defended settlements.

Otranto’s cultivated districts depend upon extensive drainage works, pumping stations, reservoirs, and maintained perimeter defences. Beyond these enclosures, forests advance rapidly across cleared ground. Agricultural wealth confers considerable political power, and disputes over water, transport routes, and food exports are frequent causes of conflict.

Morholt is colder, more heavily forested, and more sparsely settled. Towering conifers cover deep valleys beneath glacial mountains. Snowbound fortress cities surround coal-fired generating stations, district-heating plants, mineral workings, and sheltered industrial sites. Heated glasshouses provide food within their walls. Tracked vehicles and armoured supply trains connect the larger settlements, while distant outposts depend on seasonal expeditions.

The wilderness of Morholt contains extensive remnants of earlier human occupation. Marble colonnades stand among enormous roots; collapsed transit lines disappear beneath ferns and luminous fungi. Some ruins contain corroded boilers, seized pumps and collapsed industrial workings from earlier local settlements. Any surviving fragments of the original interstellar colony are exceptionally rare, broken and effectively unrecognisable. Others have become dens for native predators or sites frequented by the alien hunters. Local troops specialise in reconnaissance, tracking, survival, and combat beneath the forest canopy.

Vesalius extends from northern highlands and temperate valleys to warmer southern basins. Volcanic uplands, southern forests and violent coastal weather distinguish many districts. Rivers descend through basalt terraces and settled valleys. Offshore stone pillars and rugged cliffs shelter deep natural harbours. Human settlement concentrates along the coasts, where fortified ports support shipyards, fisheries, mineral exports, and maritime trade.

Vesalian cities rise in crowded terraces above their waterfronts. Copper roofs, stone mansions, furnace chimneys, and bronze navigation beacons overlook drydocks and armoured cargo vessels. Inland, flowering vegetation covers abandoned airfields, overgrown terraces and the weathered foundations of forgotten settlements. Expeditions seek mineral deposits and recoverable conventional machinery, supported by small settlements whose survival depends on regular coastal supply.

The original biosphere supported ordinary food webs and productive human agriculture. Long-running hunter interventions have altered roughly four fifths or more of its wild animal lineages. Some were redesigned directly; others descend from released experimental stock. Modified herbivores, scavengers and smaller animals sustain the food webs beneath the spectacular apex predators. Not every animal is a giant carnivore. Most vegetation remains ordinary to its climate: forest regrowth, seasonal mould, crop pests and waterborne disease require familiar management rather than a universally murderous flora.

These hazards sustain entire professions. Perimeter crews clear vegetation, military engineers maintain transport corridors, and specialist workers inspect filters, reservoirs, and food stores. A settlement’s security depends as much on functioning machinery and dependable supplies as on its weapons.

Human culture combines elaborate courtly traditions with industrial militarism. Noble households maintain formal uniforms, heraldic devices, genealogical records, and private military forces. Officers wear high collars, long coats, polished armour, and carefully displayed service distinctions. Ivory, deep green, bronze, and gold feature prominently among Otranto’s wealthy households, while regional forces adopt clothing suited to their environments.

Prestige also belongs to skilled artificers, engineers, physicians, and scholars. Patronage of a foundry, academy, hospital, or public conservatory demonstrates wealth and authority. Art, music, ornamental craftsmanship, and monumental architecture flourish wherever resources permit. Beneath that splendour, much of the population works in demanding conditions, bound to employers and ruling families through inherited obligations, debt, or dependence upon protected accommodation.

Human technology is unevenly distributed, concentrated in defended industrial centres and their military arsenals. Factories produce steel, machine tools, electrical equipment, industrial chemicals, combustion engines and conventional armoured vehicles. Coal and oil power thermal stations, while suitable regions exploit hydroelectricity. Wired telephones, telegraph networks and radio support communications.

A wealthy household can equip its retainers with well-made rifles, submachine guns, machine guns, mortars and artillery, supported by tanks, armoured cars and military trucks. Poorer provincial levies rely on older firearms and limited ammunition. Propeller-driven aircraft operate from defended airfields, and steel steamships and diesel vessels serve the coasts. Access to fuel, replacement components, trained personnel and transport determines military strength. Native humanity has no working spacecraft or orbital infrastructure. Exceptionally rare ancient human offworld debris is broken and effectively unrecognisable.

Rare captured hunter weapons and devices circulate as guarded trophies, military assets and objects of study. Earlier generations of human soldiers and scholars have already learned to operate some examples and partially understand a few systems. Limited repairs, adaptations and cannibalisation are possible where knowledge and parts survive; reliable manufacture of their advanced cores, power sources and specialised components is beyond the current industrial base.

Magic and the older arts are recognised features of life. Their interpretation varies between societies; local traditions describe psychic phenomena through their own vocabulary and inherited practices. Some communities place psykers under religious supervision; others employ them through scholarly institutions, hereditary service, or secretive circles of practitioners. Divination, warding, telekinesis, and ritual binding are studied with differing degrees of understanding and success.

Experiments combining machinery and psychic effects exist, usually within heavily guarded workshops or secluded sanctuaries. Such devices remain rare, temperamental, and dependent on particular practitioners. Records are fragmented, discoveries are closely guarded, and rival traditions frequently disagree over the causes of their successes and failures. Psychic practice offers real capabilities while carrying the dangers inherent in contact with the Warp.

The hunters are a regional spacefaring xenos species whose reach encompasses Malaspina and nearby systems. Their dominion is overwhelmingly vast to local human societies.

The alien hunters operate across all three continents. They arrive from offworld in advanced craft. Masked humanoid trophy-hunters employ sophisticated armour, active concealment, sensors and pulse weaponry. Their expeditions target dangerous wildlife as well as humans. Decorated soldiers, experienced wilderness fighters, and powerful psykers can attract particular attention.

The humans experience their presence as a continuing war punctuated by raids, disappearances, ambushes, and destructive interventions. The hunters treat human resistance as part of the preserve’s value. Their activity varies between expeditions: some pursue individual prey, others attack military formations or interfere with particular installations.

They maintain sites in remote regions and respond aggressively to developments that threaten their access to the planet. Attempts to follow their craft with optical observation or experimental radio detection, approach their remote sites, or organise sustained attacks against their facilities have drawn retaliation. They would resist a future challenge to their offworld access; native humans have no established orbital programme. Their monitoring combines offworld technology with informants inside human society. Knowledge can be concealed, but concentrated development is difficult to hide indefinitely.

Malaspina is held in enforced technological stagnation. Advanced observation, recovered records and human collaborators help the hunters identify research centres, arsenals and concentrations of expertise. Suppression expeditions bring small strike forces and multiple ships against selected settlements. They destroy essential industry and archives, kill or capture roughly half to three quarters of the targeted population, and withdraw. Rebuilding repeatedly consumes the gains of human ingenuity. These devastating interventions are selective; ordinary hunts and disappearances continue between them.

The shared threat has produced alliances, mutual defence arrangements, and exchanges of military knowledge. It has also encouraged secrecy, appeasement, and accusations of collaboration. Rival states continue to compete for resources and territory, each convinced that control of its own institutions is essential to survival.

Malaspina remains a divided, inhabited world with substantial industrial resources, established cultures, decayed ruins, and vast territories beyond reliable human control. Its cities preserve wealth and learning behind formidable defences, while the wilderness and the skies remain contested.


Political geography. None of Malaspina’s three principal continents is unified. States hold defended settlements, cultivated districts and the transport corridors between them; large areas within their claimed borders remain beyond effective control. Hereditary claims, municipal privileges and commercial obligations frequently overlap.

Otranto contains the largest territorial powers and strongest mass armies. Morholt favours fortress leagues and dispersed forces adapted to forest and mountain terrain. Vesalius places greater weight on ports, coastal shipping and securing narrow routes inland. Overseas possessions and treaty ports are possible, but sustaining a large transcontinental empire would be exceptionally difficult.

The powers surrounding Serravonne form one regional political neighbourhood, not an exhaustive division of Vesalius or the planet.

March of Veyrasse. Veyrasse occupies part of Vesalius’s eastern coast: several fortified ports, cultivated coastal valleys and inland mining districts connected by railway. Serravonne is a secondary port and railway junction, strategically valuable but distant from the seat of government.

A hereditary margrave rules through a charter negotiated with landed houses, municipal councils and industrial proprietors. The margrave commands the standing army and conducts foreign relations. The other institutions supply much of the money, transport and manpower, giving them considerable influence over policy and mobilisation.

Engineers receive substantial professional respect, while education and patronage offer opportunities accompanied by obligations. Workshops, land and schooling are shaped by the interests of their patrons. Railway superintendent Leont Vardesca exercises authority over railway affairs and dependants; his office does not confer command of Serravonne’s government or army.

Duchy of Caldrienne. North of Veyrasse along the coast, Caldrienne is a more centralised hereditary state with productive agricultural valleys and substantial armaments works. Its army combines disciplined infantry, strong field artillery and the region’s greatest concentration of tanks.

Caldrienne is Veyrasse’s principal territorial rival. An armed truce restrains their dispute over the Cressault corridor, while trade continues across the frontier.

Karsenne Compact. Inland beyond the coastal escarpment, Karsenne is a federation of mining towns and fortress districts whose councils jealously protect their autonomy. Its military strengths are mountain troops, engineers, demolition specialists and fortified passes.

Karsenne is an essential supplier and uneasy defensive partner of Veyrasse. Its councils resent dependence on Veyrasse’s coastal export routes and are disputing freight charges. They seek an alternative route through Calvernis; Veyrasse regards that project as a threat to its revenues and strategic influence.

Calvernis Republic. South along the coast, Calvernis is governed through a restricted franchise dominated by competing shipping, banking and industrial families. Its military strengths include convoy escorts, coastal artillery, marines and propeller-driven maritime aircraft.

Calvernis is both a major commercial partner and a rival of Veyrasse, competing for railway contracts, harbour business and influence in smaller settlements. Its potential alternative export route for Karsenne adds to these tensions.

Ceralte Admiralty. Ceralte occupies an offshore island chain. A hereditary protector shares power with a council of senior naval officers and island governors. Experienced crews, torpedo craft, minelaying and operations in difficult coastal waters are its principal military strengths.

Ceralte is a treaty partner against piracy and hunter incursions. Mainland interests frequently accuse it of sheltering privateers targeting their commerce; accusation alone does not establish official complicity.

Trade and dependence. None of the regional powers can afford complete economic independence. Caldrienne purchases ores from Karsenne; Karsenne needs coastal ports; Veyrasse purchases machinery and shipping services from Calvernis; Ceralte imports grain. Political enemies remain customers and suppliers.

Disputes over Karsenne’s freight charges and alternative export route could end in negotiation, economic confrontation or armed conflict. No outcome has been settled.

Cressault corridor. The Cressault corridor is a chain of defended valleys near the Veyrasse–Caldrienne frontier, containing a railway crossing, irrigated farmland and the pumping infrastructure sustaining them. Both states claim it. Some estates owe hereditary obligations to one government while depending on the other for transport and protection.

A previous border war ended in an armistice without resolving ownership. Frontier troops exchange occasional fire and patrols sometimes fail to return. Customs inspections and railway delays serve as instruments of pressure. Both governments accuse the other of supporting armed local claimants and interfering with water supplies.

Renewed war is feared, but neither government has publicly committed to it. Serravonne is away from this front; its railway carries associated supplies and returning personnel.

Service and internal tensions. Conscription and industrial obligations create friction within Veyrasse. Wealthier households can secure favourable postings and protected occupations for their dependants. Poorer districts supply a disproportionate share of dangerous perimeter and field service.

Railway workers possess bargaining power because transport stoppages threaten both military operations and food deliveries. The station’s stores, schedules and workshops form part of the wider military economy. Staff maintaining civilian machinery also support military transport and defensive installations.

Veyrasse military institutions. The standing army fields infantry, artillery, engineers, a limited armoured force and supporting services. It fights other states and reinforces threatened districts.

Territorial garrisons combine local reservists with permanent cadres responsible for walls, alarms, evacuation routes, magazines and perimeter patrols.

Household forces belong to major estates and industrial patrons. Charters define their obligations to the state, but personal loyalties often remain strongest.

Serravonne defences. Serravonne maintains a permanent garrison, coastal gun positions, a mobilisation depot and railway-security personnel. Surrounding districts keep reserve rolls and emergency stores. Military engineers work on bridges, drainage systems and defensive clearings.

These institutions coexist with civilian railway administration. Control of transport, stores, military units and municipal affairs is divided rather than concentrated in the superintendent’s office.

Defence against the hunters. Against other human states, armies can pursue military victory and territorial settlements. Against hunter incursions, defenders commonly prioritise preserving communications, evacuating exposed people, denying easy approaches and concentrating conventional fire when an attacker becomes vulnerable.

Fortifications offer shelter and fighting positions, but cannot guarantee protection against attacks from above. Killing an isolated hunter is one form of success; preserving a reservoir or substantially reducing casualties can be another.

Warning agreements and limited mutual assistance connect rival states. Governments still dispute whether intervention will preserve a useful buffer, exhaust their reserves or expose their own forces to attack. Their shared enemy has not abolished competition.

Collaborators provide the hunters with reports on human institutions and technical development. Publicly proven cases are much rarer than accusations; suspicion alone does not identify an informant. A settlement’s survival, a returning expedition or possession of a captured device may provoke suspicion without establishing collaboration.

Galahad’s regional knowledge. Through the first year’s books, railway conversation and local news, Galahad has learned the broad regional political geography, the states’ military specialisations and relationships, the Cressault dispute, and the significance of freight, food and mobilisation. This is contextual knowledge of the society around him.

Caldrienne extends beyond the northern edge and Calvernis beyond the southern edge; their mapped areas are portions of larger states.

Auvrienne — city record. Auvrienne, Veyrasse’s capital, rises in terraces above an extensive railway basin. Freight warehouses, cranes and furnace-lit workshops occupy the lower city. Retaining walls support roads climbing toward pale government buildings with steep roofs and narrow towers. Gun positions overlook the railway cutting. Electric trams serve the streets. Near the freight-yard exit stand a booking office and a glass-covered street plan; farther uphill are an iron-railed monument and an illuminated inn. A bookseller occupies a nearer shopfront.

The Auvrienne Collegium of Civil Engineering occupies a broad terrace behind iron railings, with a grey-stone main building, courtyard and teaching workshops. Odran Savel serves as Examiner in Mechanics and sponsors Galahad’s work. Following completion of all curricula, Galahad remains a resident scholar and occasional teacher, receiving a stipend alongside lodging and meals. The library has been read twice; specialised collections and laboratories retain separate access arrangements. Institutional and professional connections support his expanding research.

The Margrave resides and governs in a fortified palace and government precinct on Auvrienne’s upper terraces, overlooking the city and railway basin. The household occupies the palace; adjoining buildings contain the chancery, treasury and military secretariat. Court and state policy are centred there, while municipal offices administer the city. The Collegium maintains connections through engineering work and patronage.

Odran Savel lives at 37 Rue Cendravel, upstairs over a bookbinder’s shop. A side door with a brass bell opens onto the stairwell. The address lies about ten minutes on foot from the Collegium gate.

Vouressan. The Vouressan is a house-sized winged reptile capable of breathing flame. Folded wings lie along its flanks in overlapping seams; ridged throat folds hang below a wedge-shaped head, with pale mineral growths along the jaw. Its exceptionally dense white bone is known from natural histories and prized for rare weapons. Hunting accounts describe organised armed parties and substantial ammunition expenditure. Bringing one down barehanded lies beyond ordinary local expectations.


Veyrasse — family standing and currency. Noble houses stand above named common families; people without a recognised surname occupy the lowest social rank. Lineage influences access and official assumptions, even when institutional sponsorship permits an audience. Professional ability and patronage can win opportunities without conferring nobility. A stated family connection and completed legal registration are distinct matters. Veyrasse accounts use the lorrat, divided into one hundred brins. A skilled worker may earn roughly twenty-five lorrats a month.

The chancery occupies the eastern approach to the palace, joined to neighbouring offices by a roofed gallery. Bronze grilles conceal drainage outlets; carved stonework integrates with the hillside’s supporting structures. Portraits, lineage plaques and dark green leather chairs furnish its waiting gallery. Cyrrel Vaust serves as Deputy assessor for works expenditure, examining public-works claims and arranging defined technical commissions.

Pumping-network redesign: accompanied inspections expanded to every section through Cevrane. Differing daily accounting cutoffs, worn machinery and poor coordination account for the southern discrepancy; no theft is demonstrated. The submitted modular plan retains serviceable infrastructure and allows new and old sections to operate together. Each module’s estimated installed cost, including continuity arrangements, is below the corresponding annual repair allowance. Staff installed the first authorised module during the four-week petition interval. Witnessed operation preserved district supply and supported the cost and performance estimates; the next stage is authorised. Full-network reconstruction remains in progress.

Iseva Dorlac sells carved ornaments and small luxury goods in Auvrienne. Her shop presents individual pieces against dark cloth beneath glass. She sells Galahad’s traditional charms with maker attribution and retains one quarter of each retail receipt.

Valesse Orchant teaches the older arts privately in Auvrienne. Her working room contains hanging cords, narrow cabinets and brushes. Her instruction examines attention, association, repetition and the relation between sign, material and intended purpose. Galahad’s experiments with her have produced impressions that linger in some charms for hours; dependable protection has not been demonstrated.


Veyrasse — noble grants and households. Women and men may head noble houses, hold property and inherit rank on equal terms under the governing grant. Lineage, wealth, service and patronage remain decisive distinctions. The Margrave may create minor hereditary noble houses by formal grant within the charter. The recognised branch and succession are specified; land, military privileges and political seats require separate provision. Buying an estate does not itself confer nobility.

Recognised parents included in a founding noble grant may receive matching familial titles, Mother of the House and Father of the House. The titles honour their relationship to the house head, independently of their relative participation in raising that person. The House Orsival grant names Lady Corva Orsival, Mother of the House, and Sir Veskan Orsival, Father of the House. Household responsibilities and delegated authority are assigned separately. Neither familial title confers regency, co-rule or guardianship over the head’s decisions. Lord Galahad Orsival is the recognised founder and head. The grant includes 56 hectares near Serravonne with protected lawful tenancies and older buildings awaiting attention.

Planetary population survey — dated thirty-five local days before the expedition approval. Malaspina supports approximately 1.21 billion people. Regional estimates total 1,209.2 million: Otranto 757.0 million, Vesalius 340.4 million and Morholt 111.8 million. Registers are uneven, borders disputed and wilderness settlements difficult to count. These figures describe inhabitants rather than mobilisable armies. The five eastern Vesalian states together account for 68.6 million people under separate governments. Veyrasse's 15.7 million occupy a claimed area of roughly 237,000 square kilometres, with settlement concentrated in defended districts.

Otranto: crowns, republics and disputed authority. Veldrassen is an old composite monarchy whose provincial bargains constrain taxation and mobilisation. Its large industrial base supports foundries, railways, arsenals and diversified manufacturing. Ostrevain is a centralising monarchy extending tighter administration across an extensive agricultural hinterland and expanding modern arsenals. Their governments command substantial resources but must bargain with institutions within their own territories.

Rovessara is a coastal commercial republic with strong precision, electrical, optical and aircraft-engine industries alongside a diversified economy. Its financiers and merchants hold interests overseas. Brannervaux is a river federation of unequal cities and estates, negotiating water allocation and taxation; agricultural processing, chemistry and major waterworks sustain its economy. Cervaud is a militarised border duchy under competing larger patrons' influence. Veylac is a smaller industrial republic with unresolved frontier disputes.

Ossavren's former crownlands contain rival claimants, provincial armies and ports administering separate customs. Veldrassen and Ostrevain support different contenders. The old crown's population and industry remain divided among authorities; no single palace commands them all. Smaller states, clients and autonomous territories fill the remaining political landscape. Annexation, foreign concessions and limited sovereignty are established realities.

Morholt: valleys, concessions and fortress jurisdictions. Rovengard is the largest single monarchy, centred on settled southern valleys with scattered forest and mountain strongholds. Varnesk is a mining and industrial league in which councils and foundry interests negotiate control; specialist steels, bearings and durable machinery are important trades. Galdresk's wardenship combines chartered orders, estates and civilian institutions, with expertise in field medicine and communications and small houses preserving the older arts. Halskert is a river republic whose seasonal shipping and agricultural valleys sustain influential dock and mill proprietors.

Beyond these governments lie numerous fortress jurisdictions, dependent districts and settlements. Some mineral and railway concessions are financed by Otrantan interests while local authorities retain civil government. Food, heating and seasonal transport constrain military operations. Great forest claims encompass little dependable tax revenue or manpower; control follows defended valleys and supply routes.

Vesalius beyond the eastern chart. Tervayne is a western maritime power separated from Veyrasse by roughly two thousand kilometres of uplands, forests and intervening jurisdictions. There is no continuous transcontinental railway. Its shipping interests compete with Calvernis overseas; its army is not stationed at Veyrasse's frontier.

Vardol lies farther north beyond Caldrienne's northern approaches. It rivals the inland realm of Averholt, whose populated basins and provincial arrangements shape its government. Their contested frontier and internal obligations consume resources that cannot simply be directed toward annexing the eastern coast.

The Serevask Republic administers 13 million inhabitants. Its former southern basin federation linked Serevask, Varnelle, Kelbrun and Gavrel through customs, debt and water agreements; it never governed Vesalius as a whole. Reconstruction disputes following the Long Culling fractured the union. Varnelle, Kelbrun and Gavrel now control their own revenues, troops and administration. Together the four jurisdictions contain 58.4 million people, counted separately. Shared recipes, technical traditions and unresolved obligations survive their political separation; no effective federal government commands them all.

Elsewhere stand smaller states, independent ports and commercial territories. Veyrasse's security depends on its own army and revenues, difficult inland approaches, conditional partnerships and other powers' competing interests. Karsenne guards its passes; Calvernis resists a single power monopolising coastal commerce; Ceralte's maritime partnerships matter. Rivalries remain real, coalitions uncertain and defeat possible. Hunter attacks burden all governments without guaranteeing any border.

Industry and the circulation of knowledge. Industrial capability varies between districts as well as countries. The leading centres possess mature steelworking, aluminium production, casting, welding, machine tools, electrical networks, industrial chemistry and combustion engines. Conventional land armies, propeller aircraft and steam or diesel shipping depend on fuel, skilled labour and reliable maintenance. Experimental radio detection remains a developing field. Older machinery continues in widespread service alongside the most modern local equipment.

Regional concentrations of expertise arise from institutions, resources, trade and accumulated practice. Large states possess diversified economies; a famous precision workshop does not make its country the sole source of precision work. New designs may exceed current practice, while reproducible manufacture and large-scale adoption present their own demands.

Institutions of the older arts. Reliable practitioners are exceptionally rare, but accumulated records are much more extensive. Historians, physicians, scribes and instrument makers sustain schools whose few gifted members may travel between patrons. A teaching house can preserve generations of observations without producing an exceptional pupil in every generation.

Veldrassen maintains court examination offices and provincial registries. Rovessaran institutes and hospitals collect documented cases; Brannervaux's estates preserve private archives. Small teaching houses accompany Galdresk's wardens, while guarded Varnesk workshops occasionally collaborate with practitioners. Ceralte has maritime traditions, and Vesalian ports carry itinerant teachers and texts alongside private circles such as those in Auvrienne.

Scholarly, hereditary and religious schools dispute method and interpretation within each society. Divination, warding, telekinesis, ritual binding and rare experiments with machinery remain living pursuits. Ordinary study can preserve and interpret knowledge without granting every student the ability to perform it. The Collegium's limited regard for these subjects is a local academic attitude. Orchant's instruction and Galahad's observed lingering charm impressions remain part of this wider tradition; dependable protective effects remain unproven.

Planetary geography. Malaspina has an equatorial circumference of approximately 36,000 kilometres. Otranto spans tropical and southern temperate latitudes; Vesalius extends from northern highlands through the temperate eastern Marches to warmer southern basins; Morholt occupies chiefly northern latitudes. Wide ocean basins, continental shelves, detached islands and volcanic arcs divide their shores. Austral Land occupies the southern pole beneath a continental ice sheet. Exposed coastal rock, embayments and offshore islands distinguish its shore from floating ice shelves. No permanent human population is recorded on either polar landmass. Boreal Land occupies the northern pole beneath a smaller ice sheet, separated from Morholt and northern Vesalius by the North Polar Sea. The South Polar Sea encircles Austral Land. Seasonal sea ice extends beyond both persistent polar ice zones.

Mountain systems and climate. The old Otrantian backbone, Morholt highlands and Vesalian divide preserve ancient uplift, while younger convergent margins remain seismically active. Karsenne’s escarpment forms part of an older fold belt. Latitude, elevation, ocean access and prevailing winds shape the climate: moist windward country gives way to drier sheltered basins, seasonal woodland, steppe and desert. High mountains and northern uplands sustain alpine terrain, glaciers and tundra. Rivers descend through catchments toward the sea, receiving tributaries along their courses; lakes occupy enclosed basins and valleys. Hunter-modified animal populations inhabit these varied climates; the underlying atmosphere, soils and vegetation remain those of a broadly habitable world.

Annual population returns at the census epoch. The recorded combined trend is an increase of approximately 4,147,120 people per year, or 0.34 percent, across a census population of 1,209,200,000. Births and ordinary deaths are recorded for each census group; exceptional destruction, displacement and changes in living conditions alter subsequent returns. Cities are subsets of the state figures. The Cressault district return counts approximately 180,000 residents within Veyrasse’s census, without resolving the disputed territorial claim; it is not added again to the planetary total. Smaller-jurisdiction returns remain geographic aggregates, not new unified governments.


The preserve’s makers. The hunters breed, release and revise experimental animals for challenging pursuit. Human communities serve as a large test population: attacks reveal how new stock performs against firearms, organised defence and changing tactics. Humans survive through their technology and collective organisation, while the hunters preserve a sufficiently large population for further trials. Their protection of the reserve is not protection of any particular town or individual.

The lost human stock. Captured humans have also been subjected to biological alteration. Most subjects die or suffer profound behavioural and physical damage. Survivors include immensely strong, hulking feral humanoids released as quarry. Human accounts speak of savage forest peoples and solitary giants; reliable knowledge of their origins is scarce. These altered captives are not a separate naturally evolved human nation.

Hunts and purges. Ordinary trophy expeditions usually operate alone or in pairs, using active concealment, advanced sensors, armour and pulse weapons. Human culture contributes to a trophy’s standing: an influential ruler or formidable military leader carries greater prestige than an obscure merchant. Personal danger and difficulty also matter. These private pursuits differ from coordinated technological-suppression raids, whose purpose is to cripple a district and seize subjects or equipment.

Overland connections. Every charted mainland settlement connects to its continent’s wider road network. Metalled roads, gravel roads, old tracks and guarded pass routes vary greatly in capacity and upkeep. Floods, broken bridges, winter conditions, borders and attacks can close them. Island routes require water crossings. A continuous route on the chart does not imply a through railway or an uninterrupted safe journey.


Eastern Marches through routes. The principal cities and selected district centres form a network comparable in scope to the wider atlas. A northern road connects Valdrec’s approaches to Vardol; southern roads continue from Miravelle toward Kelbrun; a western pass road connects Drossane with Averholt. These are overland connections of differing capacity, not through railways. Bulk mineral freight still favours the established eastern railway. The direct Karsenne–Calvernis railway remains proposed. Dalmor’s island requires sea passages for onward travel.


Comparative staff returns. National returns distinguish annual economic production, public revenue, defence expenditure, standing personnel, trained reserves and forces supportable away from permanent garrisons. Serviceable armour, aircraft, artillery and fleet displacement are recorded separately. Veldrassen holds the largest unified industrial base; Rovessara excels in precision manufacture and electrical work. Caldrienne maintains the strongest eastern armoured concentration; Karsenne specialises in mines and defended passes, while Calvernis and Ceralte favour maritime capabilities. Veldrassen coalfields, the divided coal and petroleum districts of Ossavren, Vardol refining and Morholt concessions sustain much of the fuel trade. Local shortages can persist despite sufficient planetary production.

Staff valuations express economic flows in comparable Veyrasse lorrat purchasing power; national currencies and treasuries remain separate. Divided jurisdictions are combined geographic returns, without a single command. Cressault is included within Veyrasse accounting without settling its disputed status. The numerical register accompanies the planetary atlas.


The Long Culling. The Long Culling ended sixty-seven local years before the present return, after three years of overlapping campaigns across all inhabited continents. Reconstructed returns suggest about 56% of the pre-campaign population was killed or taken: approximately 37% killed and 19% captured, with considerable uncertainty. Captives are missing from local population returns, not certified dead. Displacement among surviving settlements is counted separately.

Major preserve-wide cullings are separated by roughly a century on average, commonly fifty to a hundred and fifty years. The shortest well-correlated interval is twenty-four years. These are historical intervals, not an announced Hunter timetable. Captured technology, rapid recovery, successful resistance and concentrated research can precipitate another campaign; weaker quarry may be left longer to recover. Ordinary hunts and selective raids continue between major cullings.

Arsenals, precision factories, research collections, power and transport bottlenecks are repeatedly struck. Enough ordinary farms, basic machine shops and dispersed practical knowledge survive for rebuilding at conventional industrial levels. Advanced recovered systems and the communities able to reproduce their workings suffer disproportionately. Destruction is severe but geographically uneven; it does not erase every book or reset all machinery to an earlier century.

Some elderly survivors remember the Long Culling directly; younger people inherit missing relatives, rebuilt streets, emergency stores and conflicting family accounts. People expect another culling within a lifetime, but disagree over whether resistance hastens it or offers the only hope. Evacuation drills, dispersed archives, refuge kitchens, hoarding, fatalism and ambitious rebuilding coexist.

Population after the Long Culling. Reconstructed returns place pre-campaign population near 1.54 billion and survivors remaining on the planet near 677.6 million. Sixty-seven years of uneven recovery produced the census total of 1.2092 billion. Fertility and survival improved more rapidly in the first reconstruction decades than today; the current 0.34% annual trend is not the rate for the entire recovery.

Veldrassen
Veldrassen is a composite monarchy whose mountain court at Cavrelisse presides over provinces ranging from tropical cultivation to cold industrial uplands. Mondessore's locomotive works and a large railway economy give the crown considerable military weight; provincial estates nevertheless control much of the revenue and recruitment on which it depends. The lowlands sell food and forest products uphill, while machinery and government contracts travel back down. Coal exports and heavy engineering sustain foreign influence. Court ceremony presents this diversity as unity, but extraordinary levies still require bargaining. Cervaud is a neighbouring buffer and customer, not a province awaiting effortless annexation.

Strengths. Heavy engineering, railway equipment and general manufactures; coal and processed fuel exports. Provincial consent slows concentration; major arsenals and railway junctions remain irreplaceable targets.
Older arts. Court examination offices and provincial registries preserve records; access varies by patron.
Table and hospitality. A composite crown includes humid lowlands, high terraces and temperate uplands; formal court service conceals many provincial kitchens. Maize polenta with beans and mushroom gravy in the uplands; rice replaces maize in irrigated lowland districts. Braised beef with red wine and layered herb pasta; provincial delegates insist on different cheeses. Plateau beer, lowland cane drink and coffee at the court. Altitude matters as much as latitude. Mountain towns import much of their grain, while court menus display produce from every province as a claim to unity.

Ostrevain
Ostrevain is an agricultural monarchy attempting to turn crop surpluses and a large population into industrial military strength. Orsevigne holds the royal administration; Tessarone concentrates arsenal work, supported by plantation and farming railways. Rovessara
Rovessara is a merchant republic governed through commercial councils. Bellacenne houses finance and administration, Avellori supplies precision instruments and electrical apparatus, and Pellavore connects both to overseas buyers. Temperate farming districts, seasonal lowlands and a dry interior give its domestic economy several distinct faces. Banks and shipping houses can finance projects far beyond the republic, but they cannot manufacture uninterrupted sea lanes or unlimited raw materials. Its strength lies in skilled production, credit and trade rather than the largest army. Inland towns consequently matter as food suppliers and customers, not merely as lesser copies of its fashionable port cities.

Strengths. Precision instruments, electrical apparatus and overseas commerce. Trade interruption threatens fuel and food imports; its skilled workforce is difficult to replace.
Older arts. Institutes and hospitals collect documented cases; evidence is better organised than practitioner supply.
Table and hospitality. Maritime commerce joins a mild western coast to upland cheese districts and distant spice markets. Restaurant fashion changes faster than village food. Bean-and-greens soup with flatbread and olive oil; tiny fried fish at the quays. Rice cooked in seafood stock, fine stuffed pasta and chilled fruit creams in wealthy city cafés. Bitter coffee, aperitif wine and citrus cordial. Fresh oil, mountain butter and imported spice coexist rather than defining one uniform national cuisine. Ice houses and refrigerated warehouses support the richest urban tables.

Brannervaux
Brannervaux is a federation of basin cities, landed districts and water authorities. Rivessole hosts common government; Molessac's pumping and engineering works turn the management of scarce or badly timed water into a major export industry. Productive cultivation coexists with dry rain-shadow districts dependent on imported food and controlled supplies. Members cooperate over transport, maintenance and defence while retaining powers that can delay a common decision. Trade with Veldrassen's factories and neighbouring agricultural states is extensive. The federation is confined to its own territories: neither its river institutions nor its name imply rule over Otranto as a whole.

Strengths. Water engineering, agricultural processing and industrial chemistry. Water allocation and estate vetoes complicate mobilisation; river freight is sensitive to damaged locks.
Older arts. Water boards retain old records of divination and flood omens beside their engineering archives. Practical credibility depends on repeated results, not an inherited title.
Table and hospitality. Unequal river cities share barges, mill customs and periodic floods. Grain, pulses and freshwater fish travel more readily than political loyalty. Lentils with smoked carp and flat noodles; poorer bowls omit fish and add greens. River-fish dumplings in herb butter and plum-filled pastries. Pear cider, mild beer and roasted-barley drinks. Floodplain gardens supply onions and beans. Fish smoking and grain warehouses make the river ports vital even to communities beyond the floodplain.

Cervaud
Cervaud is a hereditary duchy whose court and officer institutions occupy Charvessant. Vezarolle's workshops support an army unusually important to public life, but cultivated lowlands, forest produce and upland farming sustain the civilian population. The duke bargains with larger Veldrassen and Ostrevain rather than enjoying complete strategic independence. Their credit and arms help preserve the frontier while giving foreign purchasers influence. Border markets also connect Cervaud with smaller neighbouring authorities. Rank and military service carry prestige, yet merchants, farmers and workshop households have livelihoods extending across the same boundaries that officers are expected to defend.

Strengths. Frontier logistics, armaments repair and estate agriculture. Arms and credit depend on competing patrons; prolonged mobilisation drains agricultural labour.
Older arts. Border houses keep private warding manuscripts and employ occasional itinerant readers. Rival patrons make access political and discourage sharing successful practice.
Table and hospitality. Border garrisons borrow cooking from both larger patrons, while local families argue over which dishes predate foreign influence. Cabbage rolls stuffed with rice or barley and a little minced meat. Roast pork with sour cherries and crisp potato cakes. Dark beer, cherry spirit and heavily sweetened coffee for visitors. Ration bread and pickled vegetables dominate remote posts. Market-day sausages are a small luxury that survives frequent changes of uniform.

Veylac
Veylac is an industrial republic centred on Alescogne's councils, Bellorante's machine-tool works and Rionvesse's ocean trade. Municipal and commercial representation gives organised towns influence, while labour's place in government remains contested. Temperate farming districts provision cold upland factories; dry interior towns specialise in transport and practical manufacturing. Skilled metallurgy gives the republic valuable exports and military equipment, but imported food and fuel remain strategic dependencies. Ossavren's divided neighbours create both markets and frontier risks. The republic's cities are linked by production and commerce, without sharing one uniform climate, social hierarchy or relationship with factory employers.

Strengths. Metallurgy, machine tools and factory production. Exposed frontier factories and food imports limit a long war despite excellent machine-tool output.
Older arts. Factory reading societies debate arcane reports alongside medicine and natural philosophy. Private experiments remain scattered, with no dependable industrial application.
Table and hospitality. Industrial towns link cooler uplands to river farms. Working households value meals that can be reheated when factory shifts end at different hours. Baked beans beneath an onion-and-breadcrumb crust, with smoked sausage when affordable. Cheese-filled pasta browned in butter; orchard-fruit tart at civic dinners. Pale beer, plum cordial and café coffee. Cooperative dining rooms compete with private factory canteens. Imported coastal fish is popular but more expensive than the local root-and-grain staples.

Alescogne — Alescogne is Veylac's mountain capital, where elected councils, industrial delegations and labour organisations contest the republic's direction. Railways connect its administrative terraces with Bellorante's workshops and the port of Rionvesse.

Ossavren successor territories
Ossavren denotes the territories of a broken crown, not a functioning nation with one army. Ossendrienne remains a vast former capital, while provincial commands, rival courts and autonomous commercial cities control their own taxation and troops. Tressavio trades through Veylac; other districts face Ostrevain or the Seravelle markets. Coal and petroleum resources give competing rulers valuable assets, but tolls, incompatible arrangements and local fighting divide their use. Shared food, family ties and railway habits survive the political fracture. Aggregate military and economic figures measure the whole region's resources; no claimant can simply issue orders to that combined total.

Strengths. Competing provincial administrations, workshops and military supply; divided coalfields and petroleum districts. Combined rival returns; no common treasury, staff or army. Rail gauges, tolls and civil fighting fragment capacity.
Older arts. The fallen crown’s collections are divided among rival courts and displaced teachers. Competing claims to authentic manuscripts matter more than any central school.
Table and hospitality. Former royal roads carried recipes across a realm now divided by customs barriers and rival armies. Shared dishes remain a language of kinship across hostile borders. Red lentil pot with flatbread in warm valleys; barley-and-turnip broth in higher districts. Lamb with preserved lemon and almonds; former court households still serve rosewater rice pudding. Mint tea in warm valleys, mountain beer inland and sweet wine in surviving vineyard districts. Smuggling brings salt, oil and family recipes across front lines. An abundant banquet may conceal shortages in a neighbouring claimant’s territory.

Ossendrienne — Ossendrienne remains the old royal metropolis, but its palaces no longer command all Ossavren. Competing claimants, commercial institutions and district administrations share a vast city whose railway influence exceeds its rulers' dependable authority.

Rovengard
Rovengard is Morholt's largest single monarchy, governed from Arvendal and linked to overseas trade through Halsavik. Eslovanne supplies general engineering, while productive southern districts support farming, food processing and timber industries. Cold northern towns depend on transport from those warmer basins. The crown's practical task is to keep provisions and obligations moving between communities separated by difficult country. Varnesk sells specialist machinery; Halskert and Galdresk are connected through local trade and provisioning routes. Large territorial claims and a substantial population therefore do not translate into an army free to abandon domestic roads, stores and defended settlements.

Strengths. Valley agriculture, timber and stronghold supply. Winter supply and dispersed valley garrisons consume most available transport.
Older arts. Valley households preserve protective rites and stories of gifted healers. Winter isolation keeps instruction local, and court inquiries rarely reach every settlement.
Table and hospitality. Sheltered southern valleys support oats, rye, roots and dairy herds; forest preserves add berries and inspected game. Rye-crust fish loaf with potato and onion, carried cold or warmed beside a stove. Venison with sour red berries and cream, followed by baked curd cheese. Small beer, berry cordial and hot herb infusions. A winter pantry matters more than a fashionable fresh ingredient. Household drying racks and communal bake days bind city relatives to valley farms.

Varnesk
Varnesk is a league of mining councils and industrial proprietors meeting at Corsavik. Norsavia's specialist steel and machinery are valued across Morholt, and southern cultivated towns supply part of the league's food. Cold extraction districts still depend on imported grain and negotiated transport. Commercial relationships with Rovengard and the Haldrevik concessions give its firms influence beyond league borders. Industrial owners and municipal councils can agree on a profitable contract more readily than a prolonged foreign campaign. Technical skill is concentrated in workshops and training networks, not evenly distributed through every settlement or available without fuel, materials and labour.

Strengths. Ore processing, specialist steels, bearings and durable machinery. Specialist foundries are strong; grain imports and seasonal routes make an extended blockade dangerous.
Older arts. Guarded workshops sometimes collaborate with rare practitioners; no standardised arcane production.
Table and hospitality. Mining leagues inherit northern preservation techniques but rely heavily on imported grain and valley dairy produce. Potato-and-rye dumplings with smoked pork and fermented cabbage. Beef baked beneath a thick salt-and-rye crust, opened for foundry feasts. Dark rye beer; strong black tea bought through concession merchants. Canteens portion meat by shift entitlement. A late supply train can turn dumplings into thin flour soup without stopping the furnaces.

Corsavik — Corsavik is the meeting city of Varnesk's mining councils and industrial proprietors. Cold upland streets surround assay offices, technical institutions and commercial houses; railway access to Rovengard gives the league a larger market for specialist metalwork.

Galdresk
Galdresk is a wardenship of chartered orders, estates and civilian towns. Grevallier's medical and teaching institutions and Verniselle's instrument makers give it influence disproportionate to its small industrial base. Farming districts below the colder uplands help provision isolated communities; railways and negotiated access remain essential. Some houses preserve the older arts alongside practical medicine, but genuine practitioners are scarce and do not constitute a mass magical army. Neighbouring rulers value trained personnel and advice. Within Galdresk, obligations of shelter, patrol and care give institutions social authority without making every resident an initiate or every town a monastery.

Strengths. Field medicine, communications and scholarly traditions. Small arsenals and scattered teaching houses constrain scale; trained wardens excel locally rather than in mass campaigns.
Older arts. Small warden teaching houses preserve field traditions; skilled practitioners remain exceptionally rare.
Table and hospitality. Order houses preserve remedies and practical cookery alongside the older arts. Most food is ordinary, nourishing and carefully stored. Oat broth with roots, mushrooms and dried fish; kitchens adjust texture for the sick. Trout in cream with dill-like herbs, served with small sourdough loaves and berry compote. Unsweetened herbal tea and berry wine on feast days. Healing traditions do not make every herb magical. Supplies are dated and inspected; winter hospitality can impose a serious obligation on an isolated house.

Halskert
Halskert is a republic of river towns, agricultural districts and commercial authorities. Orsendal coordinates government and grain trade; Tresselund manufactures equipment for farms and water works. Productive temperate districts make the republic an important supplier to colder neighbours, while warmer pockets add different crops to its exports. Mill owners, merchants and water authorities bargain over maintenance, freight and taxation. Its transport experience supports defence, but fuel imports and seasonal conditions constrain distant operations. Galdresk buys provisions and trades specialist goods, while Rovengard is both a customer and competitor. Civilian food production is a source of power here, not background scenery.

Strengths. River freight, milling and agricultural exchange. Seasonal navigation and dependence on imported fuels limit sustained operations away from rivers.
Older arts. Pilots and mill communities preserve water omens and inherited household protections. Navigation still relies on soundings, charts and experienced crews.
Table and hospitality. Seasonal river traffic sustains mills, fisheries and gardens in comparatively sheltered valleys. Boat families carry recipes between rival towns. Barley baked with lake fish and onion in covered crocks. Butter-fried freshwater fish, green peas and a layered berry cake during the short summer. Oat beer and tart orchard cider where fruit grows. Smokehouses fill before freeze-up. Spring fish suppers mark reopened navigation and the arrival of news as much as the season’s catch.

Orsendal — Orsendal is Halskert's commercial and administrative capital, financed by productive farming districts. Grain merchants, water authorities and provincial representatives meet in a city whose railways connect much of Morholt's food trade.

Tervayne
Tervayne is a western Vesalian maritime state whose government and commercial houses occupy Tervessac. Tervassin handles ocean shipping; Brescalle builds marine and civil machinery. Cultivated districts supply provisions while upland towns provide timber and manufactured goods. Its trading networks face Otranto and Morholt across the western ocean, with only indirect connections to the eastern Marches through intervening governments and difficult country. Maritime wealth supports naval supply and overseas influence, not unrestricted inland conquest. Port families, industrial firms and agricultural districts consequently have different priorities, even when foreign merchants describe them collectively as a seafaring people.

Strengths. Maritime freight, ship maintenance and naval supply. Sea lanes carry its power; inland movement is slow and there is no through railway to eastern Vesalius.
Older arts. Port archives collect travellers’ accounts and sea traditions; private patrons sponsor a few readers, whose conflicting methods do not form a naval service.
Table and hospitality. Western ports face a different trading sea from Veyrasse and share more food traffic with southern islands and Otrantian merchants. Fish-and-potato bake beneath an oat crust, with pickled onions. Mussels in cream and white wine, followed by baked custard scented with imported spice. Coastal cider, amber beer and port-house coffee. Sailors’ inexpensive meals favour salted fish; fresh shellfish signals a short journey from water to table. Inland villages are less maritime than the national reputation suggests.

Tervessac — Tervessac is the inland capital of a maritime power, with shipping finance and government offices overlooking cultivated uplands. The Tervassin railway connects its commercial houses to the western ocean; industrial Brescalle supplies their machinery contracts.

Vardol
Vardol is a northern realm centred on Estrevigne's royal and military administration. Caldovre and surrounding cold-country cities concentrate arsenals, engineering and fuel processing; productive southern districts around Belmerac help feed them. The state can support a large military establishment but must also defend long supply routes and its rivalry with Averholt. Caldrienne is reached by the Valdrec road, not held as a subordinate province. Army procurement gives industrial suppliers political influence, while landed and commercial interests negotiate the cost. Its apparent strength therefore rests on maintaining a demanding system of food, fuel and transport rather than manpower alone.

Strengths. Northern arsenals, estate production and military provisioning; coalfields and fuel refining. The Averholt frontier and northern garrisons tie down formations; large armies cannot simply redeploy to the Marches.
Older arts. Military physicians record unusual perception when it comes to their attention. Household and estate traditions survive outside those files, often deliberately.
Table and hospitality. Refinery and arsenal towns share a strong canteen tradition with their farming hinterland. Winter provisions matter more inland than at the ports. Rye parcels filled with potato, onion and smoked sausage, baked in communal ovens. Beef in dark beer with prune gravy and steamed bread; elaborate layered honey cake. Rye beer and sour cherry cordial. Shift whistles govern supper in the industrial wards. Kitchen gardens and pickled cabbage cushion disruptions to the grain trains.

Estrevigne — Estrevigne is Vardol's royal and military administrative metropolis. Ministries, procurement offices and army institutions dominate its cold-country capital district; railway suburbs connect it to the arsenals and southern provisioning routes.

Caldovre — Caldovre is Vardol's principal arsenal and heavy-engineering city. Its railway network sustains enormous industrial wards, supplied with food and fuel from beyond the immediate cold-forest district.

Pavivaux — Pavivaux is a fortified railway city serving the Cassier and Brignan industrial districts. Military transport depots share the network with civilian suppliers working the southern roads.

Quillaux — Quillaux is a lowland fortress city covering roads into Vardol's industrial interior. Its cultivated hinterland and food stores help provision colder towns to the north.

Brignan — Brignan is an industrial city built on ice-free ground among cold uplands. The Pavivaux railway and roads to Caldovre connect its workshops with Vardol's larger armaments economy.

Averholt
Averholt is an inland realm held together by provincial bargains. Avercenne conducts common government, Rocavane concentrates mountain engineering, and lower cultivated districts supply the cold upland towns. Rivalry with Vardol competes with domestic defence for resources. Western rail links support trade with Tervayne, while Chalicchio's road reaches Karsenne and the eastern Marches. Provincial institutions protect their own stores and troops, limiting what the central government can concentrate elsewhere. Agricultural merchants, upland industrial firms and landed councils thus contribute different kinds of strength. The realm is a substantial neighbour with internal commitments, not a continent-wide power waiting to absorb every smaller state.

Strengths. Basin agriculture, internal trade and provincial engineering. Provincial bargains and the Vardol frontier absorb resources; interior transport has limited spare capacity.
Older arts. Provincial collections preserve distinct healing and warding customs. Teachers exchange access through local patrons rather than a realm-wide curriculum.
Table and hospitality. Provincial land bargains preserve different kitchens inside the same realm; grain taxes and shared fairs link the basins. Barley risotto with mushrooms and hard cheese; peasant versions use dripping instead of butter. Stuffed river pike and pastry filled with poppy seed, served when a provincial house receives important guests. Apple wine in orchard districts; malt beer in the colder basins. Public ovens are meeting places as well as fuel economies. A dispute over milling rights can be discussed for an entire supper without anyone naming its political purpose.

Serevask Republic
The Serevask Republic governs its own mountain, upland and forest districts from Serevienne. Vallorise remains an important engineering city, connected commercially to the states that once shared a southern basin federation. Varnelle, Kelbrun and Gavrel now levy their own taxes and command their own forces; old charters create claims over debts and water, not effective Serevask sovereignty. The republic retains archives, technical institutions and useful workshops, but has neither the population nor authority of the former union. Its citizens include mountain households dependent on lower provisions and lowland manufacturers dependent on cross-border customers. It has never governed Vesalius as a whole.

Strengths. Civil administration, filtration and chemical workshops, repair shops and commercial services. The republic controls only its own districts. Varnelle, Kelbrun and Gavrel have separate forces and revenues; old charter claims confer no authority over them.
Older arts. Republican archives retain fragments of the former basin federation’s case records. Its clerks cannot compel the successor authorities to supply missing material.
Table and hospitality. The old Serevask customs federation joined four southern basins, never Vesalius as a whole. Its divided successors still recognise one another’s dishes. Serevask sour rice: rice and lentils finished with soured milk, toasted seed and browned onion. Duck with bitter orange, accompanied by layered rice scented with preserved peel. Strong black tea; fermented rice drink at harvest gatherings. The remnant government maintains public grain kitchens near its ministries. Former federal recipes outlast the tax union, while each successor claims its own version is the original.

Serevienne — Serevienne is the republic's mountain administrative city and keeper of the former basin federation's archives. Its institutions now govern only Serevask; railways bring provisions and delegations from the lower districts rather than obedient officials from the successor states.

Vallorise — Vallorise is a lowland engineering city whose railways still connect the former federation's separate members. Pump works and civilian manufacturers depend on negotiated cross-border commerce with Kelbrun and Gavrel.

Varnelle
Varnelle is a delta state administered through port, water and commercial authorities centred on Varnessa. Serravole's shipping and Ceralvigne's engineering connect cultivated forest districts with overseas markets. Independence from Serevask followed disputes over reconstruction debts and customs after the former federation ceased functioning. Railway and business relationships survived that break. Merchants and water boards have considerable influence, while military establishments defend strategic approaches rather than replace civilian government everywhere. Rivessac, Kelbrun and Gavrel supply neighbouring markets. Control of freight and water makes Varnelle consequential to inland customers, but those customers remain separate political communities.

Strengths. Delta freight, customs, filtration and processing trades. Delta channels, customs dependence and disputed upstream water access constrain resilience.
Older arts. River pilots and household healers trade practical lore around the delta. Port boards occasionally investigate a useful claim without endorsing an entire tradition.
Table and hospitality. Delta rice, fisheries and freight markets furnished the former federation’s export table; incoming crews introduced sour, hot and sweet condiments. Varnelle red rice with river fish, pepper and beans, cooked in one broad pan. Crab broth with fine rice noodles and citrus leaf; confectioners sell sesame brittle. Iced citrus water where ice can be bought; tea and light cane spirit. Fish sauce is an everyday seasoning rather than a luxury. Flood years alter rice prices across all four successor states.

Varnessa — Varnessa is the administrative and commercial centre of Varnelle's delta authorities. Water offices, merchant councils and produce exchanges occupy a humid lowland city whose railways connect inland growers with Serravole's sea trade.

Ceralvigne — Ceralvigne makes harbour fittings and water-management equipment. Its railway to Serravole supports maritime customers, while the Varnessa line serves the delta's civilian economy.

Serravole — Serravole is Varnelle's principal port, with commercial quays, repair basins and passenger landings. Shipping reaches Serravonne and western Tervassin; domestic railways bring goods from the warm interior.

Vileviron — Vileviron is a forest-road market on the Rivessac approaches. Cultivated produce and ordinary manufactured goods circulate between its merchants and the communities beyond Saultac.

Kelbrun
Kelbrun is an independent state of councils and powerful plantation interests governed from Kelbrienne. Oreviano's rubber chemistry and filtration industries turn cultivated resources into valuable manufactured exports. Seasonal uplands supply grain and livestock alongside the wetter districts' plantation products. Estate labour obligations and commercial access shape politics as much as formal council debates. Serevask is a former federal partner and continuing industrial customer; Varnelle and Calvernis provide other trading connections. Army posts secure routes and production districts, but the country's influence chiefly rests on useful materials, technical knowledge and agricultural trade. Its population does not share one estate, employer or social standing.

Strengths. Upriver freight, plantation produce and agricultural machinery. Plantation levies are numerous but unevenly equipped; imported engines and fuel remain essential.
Older arts. Estate remedies coexist with workers’ inherited protective practices. Private collections are accessible through personal trust, while plantation authorities are wary of independent followings.
Table and hospitality. Upriver gardens and plantation labour communities mix basin rice dishes with tubers, legumes and orchard fruit. Steamed tuber cakes with black beans and a sharp herb relish. Pork braised in cane syrup and sour fruit; rice cakes filled with ground nuts for festivals. Roasted-grain coffee substitutes and cane spirit; estate houses serve imported coffee. Labourers eat at field shelters from wrapped parcels. Plantation owners’ lavish fruit tables conceal the uneven access to meat and purchased grain.

Gavrel
Gavrel is a group of chartered march houses with limited common institutions at Gavrielle. Each house retains its own courts and levies; Mesrienne's workshops and local markets connect their economies without erasing that autonomy. Former federal links to Serevask survive as railways, debts and commercial relationships. Varnelle and the southern cantons offer additional buyers for agricultural and forest products. Local guarantors and patronage matter to travel and trade because no single ministry controls every transaction. The combined return describes their shared resources, while actual military cooperation depends on agreements among houses rather than an automatic unified command.

Strengths. March provisioning, rural estates and frontier workshops. Household loyalties divide command; repair workshops cannot replace large losses of imported equipment.
Older arts. March houses preserve family books of omens and protections. Their secrecy divides instruction as effectively as the frontier divides military command.
Table and hospitality. March-house kitchens descend from basin customs but favour portable grain and smoked provisions for retainers travelling between estates. Millet cakes with goat stew and sour plum relish. Spit-roasted kid with nut paste, followed by dense dried-fruit bread. Sour milk, herbal tea and plum wine. Hospitality includes bread broken by the host, but its quality distinguishes an honoured guest from a hired messenger. Poor tenants substitute lentils for goat.

Bellacosta Cantons
The cantons grew out of harbour and plantation charters left without a royal guarantor after the Long Culling. Jougrenne convenes the coastal toll assembly; Nantac administers a separate inland land court. Neither can tax the other’s households. Harbour dues fund escorts while plantation owners pay for roads and demand control of the checkpoints. Veldrassen buys tropical produce and timber here, but its purchasing agents face competing canton tariffs rather than a single ministry. Tenant disputes centre on debt and access to cleared farmland; the assembly meets over commercial quarrels, not to command a national army.

Strengths. Tropical produce, timber concessions, harbour handling and coastal escorts. Canton tolls and planter credit divide the export trade. Escort flotillas answer to their sponsors; the combined manpower is not one army.
Older arts. Canton healers inherit plant lore and protective observances. Port subscribers occasionally pay to compare case records, but inland custodians resist giving away family knowledge.
Table and hospitality. Rice cooked in coconut milk accompanies peppered fish near the coast; inland labourers eat cassava cakes and bean relish. At Jougrenne, prosperous hosts serve fish wrapped in aromatic leaves, followed by cane syrup fritters. Sour fruit drinks and weak cane beer are ordinary; imported wine belongs to charter banquets.

Cavressa Principalities
A chain of small courts and charter towns occupies the southwestern approaches. Collengo’s market charter protects merchants from estate levies, while the lords around Peregia claim payment for escorting their wagons. Winter fodder and access through the uplands matter more than distant dynastic titles. Albaret brokers wool and preserved food between the courts. Marriage contracts frequently change toll rights without moving a border; merchants employ local advocates to interpret them. Southern sea trade offers an alternative to the roads, but only to houses able to finance a shipment.

Strengths. Wool, preserved provisions, upland cartage and small estate workshops. Rights of passage change between courts. Winter fodder and incompatible toll privileges limit concentration more than nominal levy strength.
Older arts. Court chapbooks mix household wards with dynastic histories. Independent practitioners move between patrons and market fairs, carrying techniques that rival courts claim as their own.
Table and hospitality. Barley noodles with browned cabbage are everyday food in the cooler districts. Shepherds carry hard cheese and sour rye loaves; court cooks serve mutton with dried plums and a crust of toasted grain. Albaret inns are known for pear pastries. Dark beer and plum spirit travel better than fresh milk.

Vaulcerre Basin Leagues
Anselleuil’s reservoir command, Jarnan’s commercial council and the estate assemblies around Votane share a drainage basin but not a government. Their water compact survived the destruction of the authority that first imposed it. Gates must open in an agreed order; delaying an upstream release can destroy a downstream planting season. Brannervaux engineers are employed as arbitrators and suspected of favouring their own merchants. Grain barges, mill repair and fertiliser works sustain the towns. Disputes usually begin as inspections, impoundments and unpaid maintenance bills before soldiers become involved.

Strengths. Irrigated grain, mill machinery, fertiliser works and inland water freight. Water commands hold separate troops. A damaged gate or withheld release can disable production without an invading army taking the towns.
Older arts. Reservoir communities preserve water-divining traditions. Boards test useful claims against measured flows; most inherited observances remain unverified.
Table and hospitality. Millet porridge with greens and groundnut sauce feeds field crews; fish dried beside the canals enriches stews. Jarnan serves spiced rice with river fish on market days. Seravelle Littoral
Astrellac’s harbour republic and the inland estate courts share the eastern littoral with smaller free ports. Their commercial convention standardises bills of lading but leaves taxes and criminal law local. Shipping families advance money against harvests; rural houses resent foreclosures by creditors who never leave the coast. Rovessaran insurers and instrument makers are influential customers. Port patrols cooperate against raiders, yet seize one another’s cargo when a debt dispute turns political. Hinterland towns depend on export warehouses for salt, tools and credit, which gives the harbours power beyond their formal borders.

Strengths. Export warehousing, coastal shipping, food processing and commercial credit. Port conventions facilitate cargo, not military command. Inland debt disputes and foreign shipping insurance expose the region to commercial pressure.
Older arts. Harbour families collect foreign protective charms and accounts of unusual perception. Commercial correspondence circulates stories faster than competent teachers.
Table and hospitality. Astrellac cooks fish in tomato and fennel broth and lays yesterday’s bread beneath it. Bean-filled pastries and olives travel inland with merchants. Wealthy harbour tables add citrus-roasted poultry and almond cakes; dockworkers buy chickpea fritters. Coastal wine is plentiful locally, while coffee is sold in small, strong cups.

Haldrevik Concessions
Concession houses hold time-limited rights to timber, minerals and fuel rather than sovereignty over every inhabitant. Asanetz keeps the surviving charter archive; Alauvenne houses one of the armed inspection posts. A house can own a railway and still owe rent to the community beneath it. Varnesk firms provide machinery and credit, exchanging technical dependence for preferred ore contracts. Charter renewals provoke strikes, armed intimidation and lawsuits over restoration bonds. Settlements outside a concession bargain for patrols in return for provisions; a company’s withdrawal can be more frightening than its arrival.

Strengths. Coal export concessions, timber, extraction machinery and contract transport. Company forces protect particular assets. Charter disputes, imported food and dependence on Varnesk equipment undermine any combined mobilisation.
Older arts. Mining crews preserve warning rites and private accounts of uncanny perception. Concession managers value a useful warning but rarely fund research that might interrupt extraction.
Table and hospitality. Workers eat oat flatbread, pea soup and smoked fish at company canteens. Mushroom pies and berry preserves mark a well-stocked household. Managers import white flour and wine for renewal dinners; crews drink malt beer or roasted-grain coffee. An interrupted supply convoy can make the company shop the only source of food and deepen wage debt.

Dreissen Wardholds
Dananske, Dreinvar and Ferorvik anchor separate wardholds along the northern approaches. Each warden owes shelter to the villages that provision a fortress, but the obligation is disputed when stores run short. Their annual muster negotiates convoy schedules and exchanges hostages against broken promises; it does not elect a king. Galdresk medical houses maintain small hospices by invitation. Imported grain is strategically more important than ceremonial claims to the iceward interior. Officers measure influence in serviceable engines and winter stores, while civilian assemblies try to keep temporary requisitions from becoming permanent rent.

Strengths. Convoy staging, cold-weather stores, fortress repair and imported-grain distribution. Most personnel guard their own supply districts. Winter fuel and food reserves impose strict limits on campaigning beyond the wardholds.
Older arts. Hospices hold copied warding and healing texts obtained from Galdresk visitors. The copies outnumber people able to demonstrate any effect.
Table and hospitality. Rye-and-oat bread, dried fish and sour cabbage dominate fortress kitchens. Venison is a costly addition to barley stew, not an everyday entitlement. Festival tables offer potato dumplings with browned butter and tart berry sauce. Hot malt drinks and strong grain spirit accompany winter hospitality; fuel scarcity limits baking as much as flour scarcity does.

Varneselle Estates
The eastern estates descend from competing settlement grants, with Varkessant’s port charter carved out of the landed claims. Estate bailiffs administer courts and patrol obligations; the port elects its own commercial officers. Fishing communities resist attempts to classify their customary shore access as a landlord’s concession. Halskert buys fish and timber and sells grain, giving its merchants leverage in disputes over freight. Family alliances cross estate borders, but succession cases repeatedly fragment holdings. Seasonal workers move between shore crews and inland workshops, carrying news faster than the formal post.

Strengths. Fishing, timber, estate workshops and seasonal coastal freight. Port and estate forces obey different officers. Agricultural limits and dependence on Halskert grain make freight disruption especially costly.
Older arts. Fishing households and estate infirmaries preserve separate traditions. Port scribes collect storm accounts; families conceal gifts they fear a landlord might appropriate.
Table and hospitality. Fish baked under a rye crust is shared along the shore; inland tables favour potato cakes with cultured cream and mushrooms. A formal estate meal adds roast poultry and sour-berry pudding. Small beer accompanies workday suppers, while herb spirits are offered to guests. The port’s fresh bread distinguishes it from outlying households that bake only when fuel permits.

Bressavelle Marches
The western marches form a belt of fortified lordships, town liberties and cultivated valleys between larger powers. Temevaux’s command guards a road junction; Malinne’s council controls a different customs district. Neither speaks for the entire belt. Tervayne merchants finance road repairs in exchange for bonded warehouses, while inland patrons subsidise rival toll houses. Small rulers survive by alternating clients and keeping neighbouring courts divided. Textile finishing, estate agriculture and wagon repair support a population far larger than its thinly charted principal towns suggest. A traveller’s permit may be valid for one bridge and useless at the next.

Strengths. Textile finishing, estate produce, bonded warehousing and wagon repair. Foreign clients subsidise rival toll houses. Local garrisons cannot be added together as an expeditionary force without renegotiating their obligations.
Older arts. Travelling readers find patrons among rival march houses. Books cross borders as gifts or contraband, leaving local collections partial and politically guarded.
Table and hospitality. Households bake bean-and-onion pies and stretch broth with torn bread. Market inns serve pork with apples and coarse mustard; better tables add freshwater fish in butter and herb sauce. Cider is common in orchard districts, ale elsewhere. Customs officers are notorious for accepting imported coffee as hospitality while charging duty on the next sack.

Vallessia Cantons
Southern market cantons rebuilt around local granaries after the Long Culling. Margeuil’s elected grain board, Darnenne’s military governor and the landed councils around Galigny compete over transport dues. Common measures for grain survived; a common treasury did not. Merchants connect warm lowland crops with cooler interior districts, using brokers who can guarantee passage through several authorities. Kelbrun buyers seek plantation produce and seasonal labour. Municipal councils resist the governors’ claim that every warehouse is a military asset, particularly after poor harvests make requisitions politically dangerous.

Strengths. Grain storage, warm-climate produce, food processing and inter-canton brokerage. Military governors and elected market boards compete for transport and stores. Requisition disputes can immobilise a nominally available reserve.
Older arts. Granary towns maintain household protection rites alongside practical medicine. Market festivals bring teachers together briefly, without creating a permanent regional academy.
Table and hospitality. Rice-and-bean dishes with sharp green relish are common in the warmer cantons; upland tables add maize bread and soft cheese. Galigny market stalls sell stuffed peppers when in season. Feast days bring roast poultry with sour fruit, and sweet fritters. Light beer and fruit cordials are ordinary; imported coffee is concentrated in commercial houses.

Margeuil — Margeuil is a fortified canton centre whose elected grain board administers public stores. Three railway approaches give its agricultural market influence over neighbouring communities without creating a common canton government.

Rivessac Coast
Saultac is the best-charted inland market in a southeastern coastal region of small port communes and hereditary agricultural districts. The survey’s single principal marker is not its only inhabited place. Pilots’ guilds set practical terms for coastal travel; inland houses control cultivated land and the roads supplying the harbours. Ceralte brokers buy provisions here without governing the coast. Rival communes share storm warnings but guard their harbour soundings. The region’s political disputes concern port fees, seasonal labour and who funds guarded access to inland markets, rather than a single national succession.

Strengths. Pilotage, coastal provisions, fishing and inland agricultural markets. Small communes lack a shared naval command. Poorly charted harbours, seasonal labour and interrupted inland roads limit the usable export surplus.
Older arts. Pilot families pass down sea omens and protective marks. Inland healers keep different records, and neither tradition offers a reliable substitute for ordinary seamanship or medicine.
Table and hospitality. Coastal kitchens combine fish, white beans, garlic and local greens; inland families make herb dumplings and barley bread. Saultac’s market speciality is poultry braised with olives and sour citrus. Hosts offer almond biscuits with coffee when they can afford it; diluted wine or herbal infusions are more usual. Fresh fish becomes a luxury only a short disrupted journey inland.

Saultac — Saultac is Rivessac's charted inland market, connected by road to Varnelle. Cultivated produce and coastal goods meet here through local carriers; uncharted villages and small port communes elsewhere in the region remain part of its population.

Karsenne Compact
Drossane hosts common business for autonomous mining councils and fortress districts. The Compact is a federation rather than a unified hereditary realm. Ores, engineering skills and defended approaches sustain its bargaining power, but coastal freight charges consume export income. Valley workshops and cultivated pockets support the upland economy. Veyrasse remains an uneasy defensive partner and vital outlet. A direct railway toward Calvernis is sought, not operating; existing roads do not provide an equivalent bulk-freight service.

Strengths. Mining, military engineering and defended-pass supply. Strong pass defence and mining; food and coastal export access depend on neighbours. Councils control separate contingents.
Older arts. Mining communities preserve protective marks, warning dreams and accounts of unusual perception underground. Council archives collect accidents; practical instruction remains within small trusted circles.
Table and hospitality. Pass commerce carried dairy and grain dishes between otherwise stubbornly independent mining councils. Food is compact enough for a shift or a winter ascent. Drossane cheese dumplings: stale bread, sharp mountain cheese and onion, browned and served in broth. Covered rye pie of smoked goat and mushrooms; the richest versions have a deep butter crust. Whey drinks and small beer during work; juniper spirit after the shift. Lower valleys supply potatoes and cabbage, upland pastures cheese. Bought flour and coastal salt become costly when freight negotiations fail.

Drossane — Karsenne’s council seat and principal mineral market occupies the escarpment network. Mining delegates negotiate freight and defence without surrendering their towns’ autonomy. Bulk ore still favours Veyrasse’s railway; the western road and southern roads offer slower alternatives, while the direct Calvernis railway remains only a proposal.

Lucaune — Lucaune is Karsenne's southern carrier town on the road to Calvernis. Repair yards and food markets serve traffic from Drossane and Maroise. The Lucegno connection offers a real road outlet, but cannot carry ore on the scale of the proposed railway.

Maroise — Maroise is a compact borough serving smaller workings and cultivated upland households. Its council oversees local supply roads toward Drossane and Lucaune; machinery and commercial provisions arrive through those larger markets.

Duchy of Caldrienne
Valdrec houses the ducal administration and principal army depots. Productive valleys support estate agriculture and armament towns; the state fields strong infantry, artillery and a comparatively large armoured force. Ducal supervision is more centralised than in Veyrasse, though estate and arsenal interests still compete for resources. The unresolved Cressault claim strains an armed truce. Northern obligations and imports of Karsenne ore prevent its government from directing every resource against the March.

Strengths. Agriculture, artillery production and armoured-vehicle workshops. Largest eastern tank arm, but fuel imports and the armed truce impose costs; offensive forces cannot strip all garrisons.
Older arts. Estate collections and military medical records preserve different accounts of unusual gifts. Ducal patronage can secure access, but also encourages custodians to conceal what they hold.
Table and hospitality. Agricultural estates feed the northern arsenals; ducal kitchens and barracks draw on the same cattle and grain districts at very different prices. Valdrec sour-pot: cabbage, potato and beef simmered with caraway; soldiers receive more cabbage and less beef. Roast goose with apple stuffing and buckwheat cakes, served at estate weddings and officers’ banquets. Dark malt beer; plum spirit poured in small glasses. Army purchasing can empty market stalls before a mobilisation. Housewives argue over whether a proper sour-pot should contain tomato, an imported coastal habit.

Valdrec — Caldrienne’s ducal capital concentrates military administration and the purchasing houses serving its armaments economy. Grain and livestock districts supply a large civilian population as well as the army. The Cressault dispute shapes travel south; the northern customs road continues toward Vardol.

Corteuil — Corteuil is a Caldrienne agricultural district centre on the Veyrasse-facing roads. Livestock fairs, veterinary yards and farm-equipment workshops serve surrounding estates. Merchants travel toward Valdrec as well as across the boundary to Salvestre.

Rocoise — Rocoise is a northern Caldrienne market town linked to Valdrec and Cervasca. Grain stores and carrier services support the wider Vardol-facing trade; its civilian interests extend beyond the duchy's southern territorial dispute.

Cervasca — Cervasca is an estate-farming borough with grain mills and agricultural workshops. Roads to Corteuil and Rocoise connect its cultivated hinterland with ducal markets, without turning it into another military or industrial capital.

March of Veyrasse
The charter balances the Margrave, landed houses, municipal councils and industrial proprietors. Auvrienne holds the court and government; Serravonne is a secondary port and rail junction. Coastal agriculture and workshops depend on inland ores and imported machinery. Railway unions can disrupt mobilisation, and poorer households bear disproportionate service obligations. Caldrienne remains the principal territorial rival; Karsenne is an essential supplier, while Calvernis and Ceralte provide competing maritime connections. The Margrave commands the standing army and foreign relations, while chartered institutions provide much of the money, manpower and transport. Education and engineering offer advancement through patronage. Railway superintendent Leont Vardesca governs railway affairs and dependants, not Serravonne’s government or army.

Strengths. Railway engineering, port trade and municipal industry. Chartered houses, municipal funding and freight bottlenecks constrain command; machinery and fuel imports matter.
Older arts. Private circles and inherited manuscripts sustain the older arts outside the Collegium’s main priorities. Proven gifts are rare; most scholars judge claims cautiously rather than treating every charm as effective.
Table and hospitality. Old coastal markets and the railway labour settlements exchange recipes with Caldrienne and Calvernis despite customs quarrels. Ressant pot: white beans, leeks and barley, enriched with smoked pork when wages permit. Railway kitchens keep it warm for staggered shifts. Auvrienne veal in mustard cream, with buttered noodles; pear pastries follow at prosperous tables. Chicory coffee in workshops; real coffee, apple cider and dry valley wine in better-supplied houses. Fresh fish is ordinary near Serravonne, expensive uphill after a disrupted train. Station households stretch yesterday’s bread into broth dumplings.

Serravonne — A fortified secondary port and railway junction in the March of Veyrasse on eastern Vesalius, reached by the hillside railway. Cleared ground, drainage works and guarded rail access protect its industrial yards. Dark stone and concrete walls overlook crowded roofs, chimneys and harbour lights. The station contains a maintenance room with benches, work clothing, a stove, a ledger desk and a wall telephone. Galahad lived under Corva’s care from his first night until leaving for Auvrienne, sleeping in an alcove adjoining the kitchen. The station was his first family home. His parents now reside at the 56-hectare Orsival estate nearby, Galahad has returned to his Collegium room in Auvrienne to complete expedition preparations. Superintendent Leont Vardesca authorised his early meals and necessary clothing on the station account. A storeroom, washroom and workshop support the station’s daily work. The alcove bed was lengthened during his childhood at the station. During the year, a hunter strike on a pumping installation farther along the line disrupted deliveries and made emergency repairs the workshop’s priority.

Auvrienne — Auvrienne, Veyrasse’s capital, rises in terraces above an extensive railway basin. Freight warehouses, cranes and furnace-lit workshops occupy the lower city. Retaining walls support roads climbing toward pale government buildings with steep roofs and narrow towers. Gun positions overlook the railway cutting. Electric trams serve the streets. Near the freight-yard exit stand a booking office and a glass-covered street plan; farther uphill are an iron-railed monument and an illuminated inn. A bookseller occupies a nearer shopfront. The Auvrienne Collegium of Civil Engineering occupies a broad terrace behind iron railings, with a grey-stone main building, courtyard and teaching workshops. Odran Savel serves as Examiner in Mechanics and sponsors Galahad’s work. Following completion of all curricula, Galahad remains a resident scholar and occasional teacher, receiving a stipend alongside lodging and meals. The library has been read twice; specialised collections and laboratories retain separate access arrangements. Institutional and professional connections support his expanding research. The Margrave resides and governs in a fortified palace and government precinct on Auvrienne’s upper terraces, overlooking the city and railway basin. The household occupies the palace; adjoining buildings contain the chancery, treasury and military secretariat. Court and state policy are centred there, while municipal offices administer the city. The Collegium maintains connections through engineering work and patronage.

Salvestre — Salvestre is Veyrasse's road market below the Cressault approaches. Provision merchants and carrier yards serve the capital road, while traffic toward Corteuil crosses into a rival duchy's commercial sphere. Cultivated estates surround a borough whose frontier trade is more prominent than heavy industry.

Maresse — Maresse is a Veyrassian workshop town serving inland road traffic. Agricultural repairs and mineral-carrier equipment share its yards; Auvrienne's buyers provide the main urban market, while Salvestre connects it with northern farming districts.

Calvernis Republic
Miravelle is the seat of a republic whose restricted franchise favours shipping, banking and industrial families. Harbour revenues, ship maintenance and manufacturing support convoy escorts, coastal guns, marines and maritime aircraft. Smaller towns supply its commercial ports without erasing rival patronage networks. Veyrasse is a customer and competitor; an alternative outlet for Karsenne could redirect freight and toll income. No agreement has completed that proposed railway.

Strengths. Shipping, banking, shipyards and maritime manufactures. Strong finance and convoy support; imported food and fuel expose it to interdiction and merchant-family disputes.
Older arts. Merchant households collect texts through maritime contacts. Private teaching and discreet patronage flourish more readily than a publicly accountable academy.
Table and hospitality. The banking ports absorbed recipes from their merchant crews, while inland households retained grain-and-bean cooking. Miravelle fish pot: firm coastal fish, onions, white beans and wine broth, ladled over yesterday’s bread. Saffron rice with shellfish and a separately served herb oil; expensive spice marks a host’s means. Dry white wine, anise cordial and strong coffee. Dockside stalls sell fried small fish in paper. A merchant’s citrus preserves may have travelled farther than the guests eating them.

Miravelle — Calvernis’s republican capital gathers shipping families, banks and industrial purchasers. Convoy business and harbour contracts sustain its civic rivalries. Roads continue south toward Kelbrun, but the proposed direct railway to Karsenne has not opened.

Bellisio — Bellisio is Calvernis's inland agricultural town on the Lelene railway. Grain merchants and implement makers trade with Kelbrun's Palesazio and Azilleuil districts, complementing the republic's maritime economy.

Lelene — Lelene is a Calvernian forest town making timber fittings and agricultural equipment. Its Bellisio railway joins inland farming districts, while Kelbrun buyers arrive from Oreviano's industrial roads.

Lalegne — Lalegne is a Calvernian agricultural road town trading with Kelbrun and Averholt. Grain, livestock and wagon repairs matter more here than the shipyards associated with the republic's coast.

Arvegno — Arvegno is a Calvernian agricultural borough supplying inland carriers. Produce merchants and equipment workshops serve the Lucegno and Serrenne roads, tying local farms to the republic's wider commercial economy.

Serrenne — Serrenne is an inland market centre on Miravelle's road network. Food packers and carrier yards assemble agricultural consignments from Arvegno and Lucegno before sale to capital buyers.

Lucegno — Lucegno is a Calvernian road town at the Karsenne trading approach. Warehouses handle goods arriving from Lucaune, and carriers continue toward Serrenne and Arvegno. Railway investment remains a political prospect, not an operating service.

Ceralte Admiralty
Dalmor is the fortified harbour and seat of a hereditary protector, senior naval council and island governors. Fishing, pilotage, convoy services and repair yards sustain the chain, while imported grain remains essential. Torpedo craft, mine warfare and knowledge of difficult waters offset limited land resources. Island communities depend on shipping rather than a mainland-style road network. Treaty cooperation coexists with accusations of privateering; no allegation proves official sponsorship.

Strengths. Coastal trade, fishing, naval maintenance and convoy services. Experienced coastal crews and minelayers; small population, grain imports and fuel dependence rule out a large land war.
Older arts. Maritime traditions and itinerant teachers preserve fragmentary practice; crews cannot rely on a gifted escort.
Table and hospitality. Limited grain land and long sea passages made imported flour, preserved fish and safe drinking water matters of government. Dalmor soaked biscuit: ship’s bread softened with fish broth, oil and onions; ashore it gains tomatoes and herbs. Whole baked rockfish under a salt crust, opened at the table; almond-and-citrus cakes for shore leave and family feasts. Weak beer, diluted island wine and lemon syrup when supplies allow. Islanders know several preparations of the same catch. Grain shortages change the size of a loaf before they change a naval ration.

Dalmor — Ceralte’s principal seat and naval harbour combines admiralty offices, victualling stores and island markets. Grain imports are politically sensitive. Local roads serve the home island; travel elsewhere in the archipelago requires a vessel.

Bellavara — A northern island port whose pilots guide coasters through the archipelago. Small repair slips handle fishing boats and coastal traders; heavy naval refits still go to Dalmor. Fish auctions and chandlers cluster above the landing, while imported grain reaches the surrounding island communities by sea.

Montelisse — A southern island harbour serving fishing villages and small orchard holdings. Salting sheds, net lofts and produce stores supply visiting coasters; fresh water and provisions are taken aboard here before longer passages. A sea connection to Dalmor carries flour, tools and outbound preserved fish. It is a working island town rather than a second admiralty seat.

Cressault corridor
The Cressault corridor is a chain of defended valleys near the Veyrasse–Caldrienne frontier, containing a railway crossing, irrigated farmland and the pumping infrastructure sustaining them. Both states claim it. Some estates owe hereditary obligations to one government while depending on the other for transport and protection. A previous border war ended in an armistice without resolving ownership. Frontier troops exchange occasional fire and patrols sometimes fail to return. Customs inspections and railway delays serve as instruments of pressure. Both governments accuse the other of supporting armed local claimants and interfering with water supplies. Renewed war is feared, but neither government has publicly committed to it. Serravonne is away from this front; its railway carries associated supplies and returning personnel. Its 180,000 residents are counted within Veyrasse for census accounting, without resolving sovereignty.

Cressault crossing — A guarded railway crossing and cultivated district under competing Veyrasse and Caldrienne claims. Estates rely on irrigation and the railway despite conflicting hereditary obligations. Customs delays, patrol incidents and water disputes make ordinary commerce political; residents are already included in Veyrasse’s census.
