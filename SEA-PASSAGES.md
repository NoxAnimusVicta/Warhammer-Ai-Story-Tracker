# Malaspina — sea passage review

Current return: **21/10/0067 AC43**. All **109 previous corridors** were reviewed. The corrected chart has **134 port-to-port sea passages**, joining all **72 charted harbours**, within **2,210 total transport segments**. Five old long-distance lines remain as through-service itineraries over named calls, rather than duplicate nonstop lines. Twenty-two retained passages have corrected alignments; thirty omitted regional connections are now charted.

This is a correction of existing-world transport coverage. It creates no new port, colony, railway, ship, expense or elapsed story time.

## What the review found

- The network was connected but often commercially implausible. Outer chains were linked around an ocean ring while nearby mainland approaches were omitted. Velisar–Valdorelle, Ordelune–Cavralto, Edrask–Cersund and the Ashalai/Merovian approaches to southern Morholt now have practical connections.
- Thirty regional links address large avoidable detours, including cross-gulf services, island-to-mainland supply and northern seasonal connections. They are not thirty guaranteed daily sailings. A connected port need not have a direct service to every other port.
- Twenty-one alignments were unnecessarily indirect. A further Istrana–Marcavisse correction removes an actual land crossing in the previous meridian fix. Routes may cross the chart seam in either direction.
- Five legacy long-haul lines concealed intermediate provisioning. Their services now use the named calls below; they are not drawn again on top of the constituent passages.
- Every existing port remains sea-connected. Seven separate railway systems, the measured Eastern Marches record, settlements, coastlines, sovereignty and census totals remain intact.

## Service and navigation assumptions

Distances follow the drawn offshore corridor on the 36,000 km circumference model. They are rounded chart estimates, not surveyed sailing instructions. Candidate paths use periodic longitude and latitude-adjusted distance. Coastline intersections are checked against the complete current land polygons; tiny shoreline endpoint differences below 0.02 chart units are within map rounding. No bathymetric survey, reef-depth model, ocean-current timetable or vessel-specific range is available. Local pilots determine actual harbour approaches.

The route search uses an approximately 60 km equatorial grid with finer polygon checks along replacement segments; it does not prove a mathematically shortest voyage. Existing safe alignments were retained when differences were small. The measured **270 km Serravonne–Dalmor** passage takes precedence over global-map estimates.

Local feeder passages ordinarily connect a dependency with its supply harbour. Regional passages carry coastal and cross-gulf traffic. Longer passages require ocean-going vessels and booked stores. Before accepting a sailing, agents match fuel/water endurance and reserve to its length, agree bunker delivery at named calls and check weather. Small refuge harbours do not automatically become full repair yards. Political borders require ordinary customs and agreements; lines confer neither sovereignty nor unrestricted access during conflict.

Routes reaching beyond approximately 55° north or south are flagged for seasonal planning. This is a review trigger, not a physical ice boundary. Existing ice-sensitive expedition windows remain controlling. No polar shortcut is assumed open merely because a geometric path exists. Commercial frequency, winter closure, local hazards and convoy restrictions can change with dated events.
