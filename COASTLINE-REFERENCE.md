# Malaspina coastline reference — Survey XII

Revision53 replaces the conspicuously smooth continental coastlines outside the original Eastern Marches with irregular coastal detail at several scales. Larger bays, headlands and narrow inlets interrupt the old continuous arcs. Northern Morholt receives more pronounced rocky indentations; roughness varies along the coast rather than repeating one tooth pattern everywhere.

This is an editorial correction to the atlas. No erosion event, inundation, new conquest, population loss or economic change occurs. The three continents retain their overall placement and recognisable outlines. The original Eastern Marches coastal survey and Ceralte remain the geographic anchor.

All 970 settlement positions, 2,182 route alignments, river paths, lake shapes, island shapes and polar landmasses are retained. Harbour points stay fixed. Coastal territorial polygons are clipped to the corrected mainland edge; inland political boundaries and national records are retained. Relief and biome layers remain clipped to land. The older coarse terrain model is not a new high-resolution coastal survey and must not be used to overwrite these corrected shorelines.

Validation compares the former and revised maps: no previously land-based settlement is newly at sea, no route or river gains a significant new land/water crossing, all revised mainland and political polygons are valid, and the protected Eastern Marches land mask is unchanged within numerical precision. Existing unresolved mapping imperfections are not silently represented as newly surveyed facts.

The proposed expedition uses the existing transport network, so coastline refinement does not change the computed travel distances or open a new transport link. Its revised schedule is a planning document, not an enacted journey.
