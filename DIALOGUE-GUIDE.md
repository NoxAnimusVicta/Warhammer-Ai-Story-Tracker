# NPC dialogue and voice

Controlling narrator guidance agreed after the estate homecoming on 19/01/0069 AC43. This changes delivery, not character histories, loyalties or abilities. The player is happy with the descriptive narration; preserve it.

## Write the person speaking

Before writing a recurring NPC, read their biography and recent exchanges. Keep a short voice note: usual register and rhythm, relationship to Galahad, what they notice first, and how their speech changes under pressure. Carry that note forward. Distinguish people through what they want to say and how they say it, not interchangeable quips, catchphrases or exaggerated accents.

Write an answer to the preceding words or action. Most people are asking, explaining, reassuring, objecting, concealing something or completing a task; they are not composing a memorable line for an audience. Let simple remarks stand. A brief answer, gesture or silence can be enough. Do not give everyone a concluding maxim or make every exchange a setup and punchline.

Use contractions, uneven sentence lengths and incomplete thoughts where natural. Do not mechanically insert stammers, pauses or interruptions to simulate humanity. Formal speakers can be fluent without sounding like policy documents. Intimate conversation should reflect familiarity rather than constant titles, ceremonial reassurance or exposition both people already know.

Humour and metaphor are welcome when the speaker would use them and the meaning holds together. Check the actual comparison, pronouns, direction of change and speaker's knowledge. Rewrite a polished-sounding but illogical phrase plainly. Never invent a shared memory merely to make a line affectionate.

## Accepted correction: Veskan and the sword

Veskan's homecoming lines were “I remember that looking rather larger” and “Though I suppose it remembers the same about you.” The second is rejected: the sword would have known Galahad when he was smaller, so the comparison reverses its own meaning. Giving the blade a memory also forces a decorative conceit into an ordinary family moment.

The player's accepted model is **“I remember when you needed both hands for that.”** This illustrates direct, familiar delivery; it is not a new claim that Veskan witnessed its manufacture or a literal limit on childhood strength. If a claimed memory is unsupported, use a present observation instead: “Looks small in your hand now.” These are examples, not recurring stock lines.

The rejected remark is superseded for character voice. Preserve the original transcript and record the correction separately. The sword remains mounted in the estate study. No sale, new weapon or elapsed story time follows from this editorial correction.

## Before sending a scene

- Does each line make literal or understandable figurative sense in context?
- Would this person say it to this listener, knowing only what they actually know?
- Does it respond to the conversation or merely decorate it?
- Have several speakers acquired the same polished rhythm, aphorisms or obligatory wit?
- Would a simpler sentence or an action work better?

Use this as an internal editing pass. Do not print voice labels or the checklist inside the story. Keep the established descriptive narration.
